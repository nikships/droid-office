# Droid Office guide

A cartoon 3D office that is yours alone: you are the only person in it. A loading screen covers the page until the office has drawn, then it fades away. Sit a Droid worker at any empty desk, watch its terminal on the laptop in front of it, and jump into that terminal yourself. Issues and pull requests hang on cork boards on the wall. There are no passwords, accounts, invites, chat or voice: your browser on the office's own machine opens straight in, and another browser on your network joins using the link or QR code the office prints when it starts (see [Install & run](#install--run)).

Every project is **a floor of the building**. Ride the elevator, pick one of the git projects you already have on the office's machine, and the office opens a new floor for it, painted its own colors. It works in your checkout right where it is: it never clones or copies a repository. Every worker, terminal, board and queue on a floor works in that project's checkout.

```
droid-office
```

## What's inside

- **A floor per project.** The first time the office starts in a terminal it walks you through picking your first project (see [Install & run](#install--run)). Otherwise you start inside the elevator, and it asks for one: pick one of the git checkouts the office finds in its workspace folder (a code folder in your home folder such as `~/Workspace` or `~/repos`, else the home folder itself; it looks up to four folders deep, so `<folder>/<owner>/<repo>` layouts work), or type a checkout's full path. **✕** or Esc closes that welcome panel so you can look around first; the elevator, or the name in the corner, brings it back, and a click on the dimmed office behind it doesn't. Only checkouts inside the workspace folder can be added. Pick another workspace folder with **Change folder** right there in the elevator, or in ⚙️ Settings → **Building**. To add another project, or go to one, walk into the elevator on the north wall and press **E**. Or click the project name in the top-left corner for the list of floors: pick one and you're there in a blink, standing on the same spot in the office. Each floor has its own desks, workers, issues and PR boards, task queue, services and pictures, and its own wall and floor colors, so you always know where you are. Reload, or come back later, and you're on that same floor, standing where you were (or where you'd get up, if you were sitting) and facing the same way. A floor that's gone by then sends you up to the roof, and a note says which one. If something is in the way of your spot, you arrive in the elevator. The elevator panel shows how many workers are busy or waiting on someone on each floor, and you get a heads-up when a worker on another floor starts waiting. Take a project off the building with the **🗑** beside its floor in the elevator: its workers stop, and you ride the elevator to another floor. Nothing is deleted. The checkout stays on disk with its workers, queue and pictures, and adding it again moves back into it. That goes for the project the office was started in (`droid-office <dir>`) too: it stays off after a restart, and the office keeps its own data in that folder as before.
- **Walk around.** Use WASD, Space to jump, and move the mouse to look around.
- **A quiet screen.** The office stays in view: the top bar holds just the floor you're on, **🤖 Workers** and the **☰** menu (or **Tab**). The menu opens every window (issues, PRs, the queue, services, search, settings) and lists what can show on screen: the workers and the floor details. Turn each one on or off there, or hide a panel with its **✕**. Pin anything you use a lot to the top bar. The choices are kept in your browser. What needs you shows up on the top bar by itself: the workers waiting on someone (click to go to them, like **N**), and **⬆️ Update** when there's a new version.
- **Coffee.** Press **E** at the coffee machine in the kitchen for a mug and a minute of quicker walking and higher jumps. A little meter under the project name shows how much buzz is left. Drink a third cup before the last one wears off and you get the jitters for a few seconds.
- **Boss office.** Stairs along the back wall climb to a glass-walled office on the loft in the corner. From up there you can look down over every desk and watch your workers go.
- **Ladder and fire poles.** The floors are stacked like a real building, with a ceiling over each. A ladder up the west wall goes through trapdoors in the ceiling and the floor: press **E** to grab it, **W** / **S** to climb, and **E** (or **Space**) to get off. Climb past the ceiling and you come up through the floor of the floor above. To go down in a hurry, walk into the hole round the fire pole (or press **E** at it). You slide down it spinning and come out of the ceiling below. The pole runs the whole way down the building, so a slide takes you one floor down and swings you off beside its hole there, ready to go again. On the bottom floor you land on a mat and a firehouse bell rings, and pressing **E** at the pole there gives you a twirl.
- **Upstairs, over the garage.** The office is the second floor. Its windows are real glass, so you can look out over the street, the trees and the neighbours. Walk out the EXIT door in the west wall and down the stairs to the street. Underneath is an open garage full of Lambos and Ferraris, and you can jump up onto them.
- **A tower that grows with your projects.** Every floor is a storey of the same building, so from outside it's as tall as you have projects: add enough of them and it's a skyscraper, a balcony off every floor, windows lit here and there at night and a cornice round the top. Up on a higher floor the street is that many storeys further down. Only the bottom floor has the EXIT door and the stairs down; the floors above it have wall there, so take the elevator down to leave.
- **Night city, and weather.** It is always night: the building is barely lit, a cyberpunk basement where most of the light comes from the laptops' screens, with the wall boards dimly lit but easy to read. Outside, the street lamps, the balcony's string lights and the windows across the street glow. The weather comes and goes by itself (clear, clouds, rain, thunderstorms, fog, and snow in winter), with raindrops on the glass, wet streets and snow settling outside. Start the office with `--city Berlin` and the sky follows that city's live forecast instead. Every window shows the same sky, and **⚙️** → **Building** shows what it's doing.
- **The balcony.** Glass doors on the south wall slide open onto a balcony over the garage, with string lights, a bench and a bistro table.
- **Take a seat.** Press **E** at the lounge couch, a beanbag, the balcony bench or a stool at its bistro table, or the couch or the boss's chair up in the boss office to sit down. Walk off, jump or press **E** again to get up.
- **Minesweeper on the boss's monitor.** In the boss's chair, **E** plays Minesweeper on the monitor: the camera moves in close and the board sits right on the screen. Click to dig, right-click (or Shift-click) to flag, and click a number to dig around it once its mines are flagged. The first click is never a mine. **Stop playing** leaves your game up on the monitor, with its clock paused, until you come back. Walk off to get up.
- **Pick your character.** The first time you open the office, a character select screen lets you choose your skin tone, hair style, hair color and shirt, with a spinning preview. **✕** or Esc goes in with the look and name picked so far. Change it any time from **⚙️** → **You**. Settings is a wide window with categories down the side — You, Sound, Notifications, Building, Factory, Workers, Subagents and Phone — and on a phone that list is a row across the top. It reopens on the last category, arrow keys (and Home and End) move between them, and each setting says whether it is just you, this floor, or the whole building.
- **Hire workers.** Walk up to an empty desk and press **E** to hire a Droid worker, or press **P** to write a task first. A little worker sits down, a laptop opens, and the agent's live screen appears on it. Pick the model and reasoning effort for each queue task too.
- **Bean bags for the overflow.** There are 16 desks. Once they're all taken, a bean bag comes out on the floor with a low lap desk in front of it. Hire a worker there the same way as at a desk, and the next bean bag comes out, up to 12 around the room. The task queue seats workers on them too. A bean bag stays out while a worker sits on it, and the empty one goes away again as soon as a desk frees up.
- **Shells.** Press **B** at an empty desk to open a plain login shell for dev servers, git or tests. It works like any worker's terminal: open it from another window and it streams there too.
- **Guests: agents you run yourself.** Start `droid` (or `claude`, `codex`, `opencode`, `grok`, `muse`) in a terminal of your own, in one of the floors' checkouts, and within a few seconds a guest sits down at a free desk on that floor with a name tag, its provider, and for droid its model, session title and status. Nobody hires it. It counts on the top bar, under **N** and in the elevator's per-floor counts like any worker, and goes home when its process exits. The office only watches a guest: it has no terminal in the office, so **E** at its desk shows what the office knows (the pid, the terminal it's on, its folder and where its status comes from) and takes no typing. It's never sent home, prompted, handed a task, shot, pruned or counted against the worker limit; type to it and quit it in its own terminal. To work with a droid guest in the office instead, **bring it in**: **R** at its desk (or **🚪 Bring it in** in its window) quits its droid outside, whose terminal goes back to the shell, and the same session carries on at the same desk, with the same name, as one of the office's own workers, its terminal opening right away. That works for a droid guest in the floor's checkout itself whose session the office knows, between turns; otherwise its window says why not. A droid guest's status comes from its session transcript: *working* mid-turn and *done* once its turn ended. A permission prompt looks like working from there, and a guest whose transcript the office can't tie to it (another CLI, or two droids in one folder) just shows *running*. A droid you start by hand in a worker's own worktree is that worker's: its desk hint says so instead of seating a second guest.
- **Isolated branches.** When you hire with a task, you can tick *own git worktree*. The worker then gets its own `office/<name>` branch under `.droid-office/worktrees/`, so parallel workers never share a checkout. The branch starts from the latest on the forge (`git fetch origin <branch>`, then `origin/<branch>` when the checkout is behind), so a task includes pull requests merged since you last pulled. The floor's own checkout is never moved. If it checks out or renames a branch of its own, the office follows that branch for the pull request and for sending it home. A branch that was already there, like main, is never deleted, and commits still only on the office branch are kept. When you send that worker home you choose whether to keep the worktree and branch, delete the worktree, or delete both, and the office warns you first about uncommitted changes or commits no remote has. `droid-office prune` clears out whatever was kept, once it is safe. Under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Workers whose pull request merged*, have them go home by themselves once their pull request (or GitLab merge request) merges: as soon as one isn't working or waiting on you and none of your windows has its terminal open, it leaves and its worktree and branch are deleted. Uncommitted changes, or commits that aren't on the remote, keep the worktree, as when you send it home. It's off until you turn it on, and turning it on sends home the ones already merged.
- **One task across several projects.** When you hire a worker in its own worktree, **🗂️ Also work in** lists the building's other floors: pick the projects the task spans (in the hire dialog, or in **🤖 Hand to a worker** / **✍️ Ask a worker** from the boards). The worker starts in a workspace folder with a worktree of each project, all on the same `office/<name>` branch, and a short brief (`AGENTS.md`, the **🗂️ Across repositories** prompt in Settings) saying which folder is which. **O** opens a pull request in every project it committed to, and each description lists the others. The issue the task came from is closed by the home floor's pull request and mentioned from the others. Press **O** again for each project's pull request. The Changes window has a tab per project. Sending the worker home checks and deletes every worktree, and *Workers whose pull request merged* waits until every project's pull request has merged.
- **One-click PRs.** When a worktree worker is done, press **O** at its desk. The office pushes the branch the worktree is on (including one the worker created itself) and opens a pull request with a title and body drafted from the task (work handed over from the issues board gets a `Closes #n`). The PR shows up on the PR board right away, tagged with the worker's desk, and **Go to desk** takes you there. Press **O** again to see the PR.
- **Live terminals.** Press **E** at an occupied desk to open the real terminal (a PTY, over WebSockets). Open the same one from a second tab or another browser and it streams in each; closing one window leaves the others attached. Drop a file onto the open terminal, or paste a screenshot, and the office keeps it under `.droid-office/drops/` and types the path in, the way a native terminal does with a dragged file. The PTY takes the size of whichever window typed into it last.
- **Pictures in prompts.** Paste a screenshot (or drop picture files onto the window) in any prompt that goes to an agent: hiring a worker, prompting one, a board agent or the Team lead, **🤖 Hand to a worker** / **✍️ Ask a worker**, the task queue's *New task* box and the meeting room's *What's it about?* box. Each picture shows as a small numbered thumbnail under the box, and a ✕ appears when you hover it; removing one renumbers the rest. PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, up to 10 per prompt and 25 MB each. A picture alone is enough to send. The agent gets your text, then a blank line and one line per picture, like `Image 1: /path/to/shot.png`, so you can say "image 2" and it knows which; it opens the files itself. Meetings give every worker at the table the same pictures. The office keeps each picture under `.droid-office/drops/staged/` from the moment you paste it and deletes it when you take it out or close the window without sending. Once the prompt is sent, each worker has its own copy under `.droid-office/drops/<worker id>/`, which goes when the worker does. A queued task keeps its pictures until it starts or you remove it. The *Run in shell*, commit and pull request boxes take no pictures, since they aren't prompts to an agent.
- **Changes at the desk.** Press **C** at an occupied desk (or click **🌿 Changes** in its terminal) to see what the worker changed: the changed files and their diff against the newer of the branch the office was opened on and that branch on the remote, refreshed every couple of seconds while it works. A worker across several projects has a tab per repository, each against that floor's own branch. From there you can commit, discard, or push the branch and open a pull request.
- **Live status.** Droid's hooks drive each worker's status: *working*, *needs input* or *done*. When a worker needs a human or has finished, it jumps up and down and you hear a ding. Its antenna bulb shows the status from across the room.
- **Workers act out what they're doing.** A working worker acts out its latest tool call, so you can tell what it's doing from across the room without reading its card. Reading files, it holds up a stack of papers and flips through them. Editing, it hunches over the keys and types flat out. Running tests or a build, it leans back with its hands behind its head. Searching or fetching the web, a globe spins beside its laptop. When its tests or build fail twice in a row, it puts its head in its hands. Waiting on you, it jumps for a couple of seconds, then crosses its arms and taps its foot. When it finishes, it does a little spin and a puff of confetti goes up. Droid's hooks tell a shell command that runs tests from one that only reads, and they report failures.
- **Next worker that needs you.** Press **N** to go straight to the worker that has waited longest on someone (it needs input, or it's done and nobody has looked), standing behind it and looking at its laptop. Press **N** again for the next one, oldest first, and after the last you start over. A pin at the edge of the screen points the way to each waiting worker you can't see, red for *needs input* and green for *done*, and the top bar counts them (`🙋 2 waiting · ✅ 1 done`; click it to do the same as **N**). With nobody waiting on your floor, **N** tells you which floor has someone.
- **Notifications.** You don't have to be in the office tab. The tab title counts the workers waiting on someone, like `(2) my-project · Droid Office`. Allow desktop notifications (the office asks when you first hire a worker, or turn them on under **⚙️** → **Notifications**) and a worker that needs input or finishes while you're in another tab or app pops one up. Click it to jump straight to that worker's terminal. For your phone or chat app, paste a Slack or Discord incoming webhook under **⚙️** → **Notifications** → *Channel notifications* (or start with `--webhook <url>`), and the office posts to that channel when a worker has needed input or been done for a few seconds with none of your windows at its terminal.
- **Task cards.** A card over each worker's head names what it's on ("Fix Login Redirect") and says in one line what it's doing right now. A small model (Haiku, through `droid exec` — the Droid the office already needs) writes it from the worker's prompts and recent tool calls. Without it, the card falls back to the prompt itself.
- **Survives restarts.** Workers are saved to disk with their model choice and session ID, and their terminals run in a small process of their own. When the server restarts (a dev-server reload, a `kill` of its process), the agents keep working through it, and the new server picks every terminal back up, scrollback and all. Open terminals reconnect by themselves. Ctrl+C stops the workers; the next start wakes them back up in their sessions, and **R** resumes the Droid session of one that exited. When a restart does take a worker's terminal with it (the machine rebooted, the terminal process died or was replaced by a newer one), a worker that was in the middle of something is resumed with *continue*, so it carries on by itself. Each worker's last 3,000 lines of terminal are kept too, so after a restart (or a crash) you can still scroll back through what the workers printed.
- **Search.** Press **/** (or **🔎 Search** in the **☰** menu) to search every worker's terminal at once, including what they showed before the office restarted. Click a terminal line to open that terminal scrolled to it, with the line lit up.
- **Command palette.** Press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a Mac) and type a few letters to find a worker, an issue, a pull request, a service, a board or an action. **Enter** opens it. **Shift+Enter** walks you there first, then opens it. Up on the roof, or when it isn't a place you can stand, **Shift+Enter** opens it the same way **Enter** does.
- **Compute wall.** A big screen on the west wall, between the exit door and the kitchen, shows how busy the office's machine is on its left end: CPU and memory right now, the last five minutes of each, and how many workers the office runs. The rest of it is your Factory Droid Computers once the office is connected to Factory (below): a tile per computer with its status (active, asleep, provisioning with the step it's on, waking, error), whether Factory runs it or it's your own machine (BYOM), and for a managed one its CPU, memory and disk now and over the last six hours. When the office's own machine is one of them (a BYOM computer named after it), the machine's end wears a *Droid Computer* badge instead of it getting a tile of its own, and the window marks it *This machine*. Up to six computers get big tiles, then they go to compact rows. Not connected, it says how to connect; if Factory is down or turns the key away, it keeps the last computers it read with their age. The cubes in the rack under it are your computers too, lit when they're active.
- **Droid Computers window.** Walk up to the compute wall and press **E** (or **☰** → **Computers**, or the command palette) for every Droid Computer: its status, provider, load and repositories. Click one for its details, its provisioning steps with how long each took, and CPU, memory and disk charts over 6 hours, 24 hours or 4 days. A managed computer has **Wake** (resume it if it's asleep), **Reboot**, **Keep awake**, **Refresh credentials** (its git credentials and the organization's secrets), **Retry install deps**, rename and remote user, and **Delete**, which takes its name typed out. **+ New computer** makes one, or several at once with a name prefix, picking the repositories it clones from those Factory's GitHub app sees (this floor's repository is picked to start with). Under the list, **Computer secrets** are the environment variables every computer in the organization gets: their names, a way to set one and remove one. Values are write-only and never shown.
- **Worker limit.** Start with `--max-workers <n>`, or set a limit under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Worker limit*, and the office runs at most that many workers at once across every floor (shells and board agents count too). Hiring past it is refused with a message saying why, and the task queue waits for room instead of failing its tasks. In the office, the limit can be lowered but never raised past `--max-workers`. When memory is 90% used, or the CPU has been 90% busy for half a minute, the compute wall says the machine is under pressure, the hint at an empty desk says so, and the hire dialog warns you before you hire.
- **Issues board.** A tack board shows GitHub issues in *Open*, *In progress* and *Closed*. Click a column's header to filter it by label: pick one or more and the column shows only the cards with any of them. Your filters stay put for each floor, in your browser. Or type in the box under a column's header to narrow it to the cards whose titles have every word you type (any case, any order). It works along with the label filter, the count shows how many of the column's cards match, **✕** clears the box, and the boxes start empty each time you open the board. Click an issue to read it and its comments, or add a comment of your own (it posts as the account `gh` is signed in as on the server; the window names it), then choose **Hand to a worker** to seat a worker with a ready-made prompt, or **Add to queue**. Or take its card straight off the board, without opening it: point the crosshair at the note and press **E** or click (**O** opens it instead). **✋ Pick it up** in an issue's window does the same. You carry the card across the office in your hands, and it stays missing from the board until you put it back: press **E** at an empty desk to hire a worker for it, at a worker's desk to hand it to that worker, or at the task queue to queue it. A worker that takes the card gets the issue assigned on GitHub, so it moves to *In progress*. **Q** puts the card back. **Close issue…** closes it as completed or not planned, with an optional comment, and takes it off the queue if it was waiting there. **Edit** next to an issue's labels (**Add labels** when it has none), or the **Labels** button that shows when you hover its card on the board, picks its labels from the repo's own: tick them on and off, filter by typing (**Enter** ticks the first match), then **Save labels**. The change goes out through the server's `gh` or `glab` account. An *in progress* (or *doing*, *wip*) label moves the issue to *In progress*.
- **Jira board.** Connect the office to Jira Cloud once, under **⚙️** → **Building**, with a site, an email and an [API token](https://id.atlassian.com/manage-profile/security/api-tokens). A read-only token (scope `read:jira-work`) is enough, since the office only reads Jira. Then give a floor a Jira epic (for example `EDP-168`), and that floor's issue board gets a **Jira** tab next to **Issues** with all of the epic's tickets in *To Do*, *In Progress* and *Done*. The 📌 board on the wall has the same two tabs across its top: point at one and press **E** (or click) to switch what the wall shows, and **E** on a ticket there opens it. The board window opens on whichever tab the wall is showing. Each card shows its key, summary, type, priority, assignee and status, and links to Jira. Click one to read its description and comments, or choose **Hand to a worker** to seat a worker with the ticket as its prompt. The office never changes a ticket; tell a worker to update Jira itself (with `jtk`, say) if you want that. A floor without an epic has the issue board it always had.
- **Factory.** Connect the office to your [Factory](https://factory.ai) account once, under **⚙️** → **Factory**, with an [API key](https://app.factory.ai/settings/api-keys). The card then says whose key it is (name and email), shows the key only as `fk-…` and its last four, who connected it and when, and what it can reach, one line each: ✓ Droid Computers, Droid sessions, CI automations, AutoWiki and the organization, ✗ with why for anything it can't (service accounts need a Teams plan). **Check again** asks Factory afresh, **Replace key** swaps in another, and **Disconnect** forgets it. If Factory turns the key down later (it was deleted, say), the card says *Key rejected* and the office stops reading Factory until you check it again or replace it. While connected, the office keeps your Factory computers and their load, your recent Droid sessions, your CI workflows and runs, and your AutoWiki runs up to date for its boards and windows.
- **CI automations board.** On the north wall past the gong, under the Factory mural: every repository where Droid runs in GitHub Actions, this floor's first and marked, each Droid workflow as a chip (code review, security review, @droid, scheduled…) with what it runs on, colored by how its latest run went, and the week's runs, passes and failures along the bottom. Press **E** at it (or pick **CI automations** in the ☰ menu or the palette) for the window: every workflow with all Factory knows about it (file, triggers, schedule, model, inputs, who last changed it, your access) and a link to it on GitHub, the recent runs (result, event, the PR or commit, when and how long), and the workflow pull requests Factory opened. **+ Add a workflow** adds Droid code review (deep or shallow, a security review too, model and effort) or a Droid job of your own (a prompt, the events and a schedule) to this floor's repository or any other Factory's GitHub app can see; **Change** and **Remove** do the same for one that's there. Each one is a pull request Factory opens in the repository, linked in the window; nothing changes until it merges. **Rescan** looks through the repositories again past Factory's 30-minute cache.
- **Cloud workers.** With the office connected to Factory and an active Factory computer, the hire dialog has **Runs on**: *💻 This machine* (the default) or a computer (*☁ orb · e2b*). Pick a computer and say which folder on it the session starts in (empty is its home folder, `~/repo` works on a managed one); the dialog remembers the last folder per computer, and hides the worktree and other-repository options, which don't apply there. The worker's Droid session runs on that computer through Factory, in auto mode at the office's usual autonomy (`--auto` in `--agent-args`, else high), with the model and effort picked; your own (BYOK) models only run on this machine. The dialog stays open while the session is made (it can take 15 seconds or more), and when Factory says no it says why and nobody is hired. A cloud worker sits at its desk like any other, with ☁ and its computer on its card, its desk hint and its 🤖 Workers row, and acts out its latest tool call. E opens its window: where it runs and the credits it has used, its session's transcript (the same one the Sessions window shows, read every few seconds while it works, and *Starting…* while Factory is still making the session), a link to the session in Factory's web app, and a prompt box (Enter sends, pictures pasted or dropped) with **Interrupt** while it works. It has no terminal, worktree or Changes window here, can't be shot, and doesn't count toward the worker limit. X sends it home: it's interrupted if it's working, and its Factory session is kept unless you tick *Also delete its Factory session*. When its session or computer is gone from Factory it stays at its desk saying so until you send it home.
- **PR board.** A second tack board shows pull requests in *Draft*, *In review*, *Approved*, *Merged* and *Closed*, with CI status and diff size. Its column headers filter by label too, and the box under each header narrows that column by title the same way. Click a PR to read its description, comments, reviews and checks, and comment on it. **Files changed** shows the diff, with the files listed on the left as a folder tree or a flat list, and review comments under the lines they're on. Tick a file **Reviewed** (or press **V**; **J**/**K** step through files) and it folds away. Your ticks are kept in your browser, and a file you ticked that changes again is flagged. **Merge…** squashes, merges or rebases it, and can have GitHub merge it once its checks pass. **Close PR…** closes it without merging, with an optional comment, and can delete its branch. **Review** and **Fix comments & merge** hand the PR to a worker; the second one addresses the review comments, gets the checks green and merges. When the PR conflicts with its base, that button becomes **Fix conflicts & merge**: a new worker is picked by default, merges the base in, resolves the conflicts and merges. A PR's (or MR's) labels change the same way as an issue's: **Edit** in its window, or **Labels** on its card.
- **The merge gong.** A brass gong stands by the elevator, on the far side from the PR board. When a pull request merges, it rings, confetti rains down from the ceiling all over the floor (the loft too) and bursts over the desk the PR came from (with no worker there, over the gong), and every worker that's awake climbs up on its desk for a quick dance, its light flashing like a disco ball, before it hops back into its seat. Workers on bean bags dance on the bag, and the board agents on their kiosks. It rings however the PR merged: from the PR window, by a worker's `gh pr merge`, by auto-merge or on GitHub itself. When the last task on the task queue gets done, the gong rings three times and confetti flies over every desk. Walk up and press **E** to bang it yourself.
- **Ask a worker about anything on a board.** Every issue and PR has **✍️ Ask a worker…**: type your own prompt, and the worker gets it along with which issue or PR it's about. Send it to a new worker at a free desk, or to one already sitting at a desk.
- **Board agents.** An agent stands at a little kiosk beside each of the issues board, the PR board and the task queue. Walk up and press **E**, type what you want, and it runs as a Droid agent that already knows its job. The issues agent files, finds, triages, labels and closes issues with `gh`. The PR agent sums up, reviews, comments on and merges pull requests. The queue agent turns what you ask for into tasks on the queue, one per piece of work, and can tell you what's queued or take tasks off. ## Requirements

On the machine that runs the office (your laptop or a VPS):

- **Node.js 20+**. Prebuilt PTY binaries ship for Linux, macOS and Windows, x64 and arm64, so no compiler is needed.
- The **[Droid CLI](https://docs.factory.ai/cli/getting-started/quickstart)** (`droid`), installed, signed in and configured as the user that runs the office. Every worker, board agent and meeting seat is a Droid session.
- **git**, plus the **GitHub CLI** (`gh`) logged in (`gh auth login`) for GitHub projects' issue and PR boards, and the **GitLab CLI** (`glab`) logged in (`glab auth login`) for GitLab projects. A GitLab floor's PR board shows its merge requests, and its workers open merge requests with `glab`. Self-managed GitLab instances work once `glab auth login --hostname <host>` has signed in to them.
- `curl` is optional. The status hooks use it when it's there and fall back to Node when it isn't.

## Install & run

One line installs the latest release and starts the office. You don't need to clone anything:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikships/droid-office/main/install.sh | bash
```

Anything after `bash -s --` goes to the office, such as a port:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikships/droid-office/main/install.sh | bash -s -- --port 4700
```

The script checks for Node.js 20+ and npm, downloads the newest [release](https://github.com/nikships/droid-office/releases) into `~/.local/share/droid-office` and installs its dependencies there. It also puts an `droid-office` command in `~/.local/bin`, so after the first run `droid-office` on its own starts the office. Run the curl line again to update. Set `DROID_OFFICE_VERSION=v0.1.68` to install a particular release, or `DROID_OFFICE_INSTALL_ONLY=1` to install without starting. The other settings are listed at the top of [`install.sh`](install.sh). It runs on macOS and Linux.

**On Windows**, one line in PowerShell does the same:

```powershell
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikships/droid-office/main/install.ps1 | iex
```

To pass the office options, like a port, run it as a script block instead:

```powershell
& ([scriptblock]::Create((irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nikships/droid-office/main/install.ps1))) --port 4700
```

It installs releases into `%LOCALAPPDATA%\droid-office` and puts an `droid-office` command in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\droid-office\bin`, which it adds to your user PATH. Open a new terminal after the first install and `droid-office` starts the office from PowerShell, cmd or Git Bash. Run the irm line again to update. It takes the same settings as `install.sh`, set the PowerShell way (`$env:DROID_OFFICE_VERSION = 'v0.1.68'` before the irm line), plus `DROID_OFFICE_NO_MODIFY_PATH=1` to leave your PATH alone, and `DROID_OFFICE_BIN_DIR=none` skips the command. They're listed at the top of [`install.ps1`](install.ps1). It works in Windows PowerShell 5.1 and PowerShell 7. You can also run `install.sh` inside WSL.

Or install from a clone, to work on the office itself:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/nikships/droid-office && cd droid-office
npm install          # also builds the client and server
npm install -g .     # puts `droid-office` on your PATH
```

Then run it, from anywhere:

```bash
droid-office
```

The first time it starts in a terminal, it walks you through setting up before it opens:

1. **Which folder your projects are in.** It suggests a code folder you already have (`~/Workspace`, `~/code`, `~/repos`…), else your home folder. The office looks for git projects in it and its subfolders, and never clones or copies anything.
2. **Your first project.** It lists the git projects it found, most recently active first. Pick one by number, type its full path, or type a word to search. It becomes the first floor, and you can add more the same way.

Press Enter to skip any step: the elevator in the office asks for your first project too. Run `droid-office setup` to go through it again while the office is stopped, or `droid-office setup --projects ~/Workspace --project ~/Workspace/my-project` to do the same without questions, from a script. When `install.sh` is piped into bash, it hands the office your terminal so the walkthrough can ask its questions.

It prints the URL for this machine's browser, which needs no login, and — unless bound to loopback — a join URL and QR code for other devices on your Wi-Fi (see [Access](#access) below).

The office keeps its data in `~/droid-office` (`--home` or `DROID_OFFICE_HOME` to move it) and opens your existing checkouts as floors where they are. To make it look for them in another folder, like `~/Workspace`, pick the **Workspace folder** in ⚙️ Settings → **Building**, or with **Change folder** in the elevator's add-a-project panel (or start with `--projects` or `DROID_OFFICE_PROJECTS`). The folder has to exist. Floors you already have stay where they are when it moves. The list of floors is `~/droid-office/.droid-office/floors.json`. Each floor keeps its workers, queue, pictures and worktrees in its own checkout's `.droid-office/`.

Every setting the office and the installers read from the environment is listed, with its default, in [`.env.example`](.env.example). The office doesn't load `.env` files: export the settings in the shell that starts it, or set them as `Environment=` lines in its systemd unit. A command-line flag wins over its environment variable.

You can also start the office in a project, `droid-office ~/code/my-project`: that project becomes a floor, and the office keeps its data in `~/code/my-project/.droid-office` as it did before there were floors. An office that already ran in a project carries on in it when you start `droid-office` there again. You can take that project off the building in the elevator like any other floor.

### The Mac app

On a Mac with Apple Silicon, **Droid Office.app** does all of this for you: it starts the office, opens it full screen, and updates itself. Download `Droid-Office-<version>-arm64.dmg` from the [latest release](https://github.com/nikships/droid-office/releases/latest), open it and drag **Droid Office** into Applications. It's signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens without a warning. It needs no Node.js of its own (the office runs on the app's built-in Node), but it does need `droid`, `git` and, for the GitHub boards, `gh` signed in (`gh auth login`).

- **The first launch** asks where your office lives: `~/droid-office` (what `droid-office` opens with no folder), or a folder you pick. Pick a folder an office already ran in to carry on with its floors and workers. **Office → Change Office Folder…** switches later. The app remembers the choice in `~/Library/Application Support/com.nikships.droid-office/settings.json`, which can also set `"port"` (default 4600).
- **Starting.** The app starts the office with `droid-office --home <folder>`, or `droid-office <folder>` for a checkout an office was started in as a project, on port 4600, and shows it once it answers. If an office already answers on that port (a `droid-office` in a terminal, say), the app opens that one instead of starting a second. If something else holds the port, it takes the next free one. The office and its workers get your login shell's environment, as they would from a terminal, rather than the bare one macOS gives apps: its `PATH`, and everything else your shell profile exports (`JAVA_HOME`, API keys, `LANG`…). Without a locale from anywhere, they get `LANG=en_US.UTF-8`.
- **Opened from the disk image or Downloads**, the app offers to move itself into Applications. macOS runs it from a read-only copy there, so it can't update itself, and workers' terminals stop working once the disk image is ejected.
- **Full screen.** The window opens full screen. **Ctrl+Cmd+F** (or **View → Toggle Full Screen**) leaves it and goes back. Links to issues, pull requests and docs open in your browser; **Office → Open in Browser** opens the office itself there too.
- **Quitting** works like restarting the office: workers keep running in their terminal host, and opening the app again within 30 minutes picks them back up. Closing the window leaves the app and the office running; click the Dock icon to bring it back. The office's output goes to `~/Library/Logs/Droid Office/office.log` (**Office → Show Office Log**).
- **Updates.** Every release on `main` comes with the app (see [Releases](#development)). The app checks for a newer one at launch and every 30 minutes, downloads it in the background, and asks to restart once it's ready: restarting keeps your workers running, and **Later** installs it the next time you quit. **Droid Office → Check for Updates…** checks now. It reads the public releases of `nikships/droid-office`, so it needs no GitHub login. What it did is in `~/Library/Logs/Droid Office/updates.log`.

### Install as an app

In Chrome or Edge, open the office and choose **Install** in the address bar, or **⋮ → Cast, save and share → Install page as app**. Droid Office opens in its own window, with its own icon in your Dock, taskbar or app launcher. This needs HTTPS with a certificate your browser trusts, or `localhost` (including an SSH tunnel). ### Access

There are no office passwords, accounts, sessions or login pages. Each start mints a random LAN token, kept in memory and never written to disk; starting the office prints the join URL and a QR code of it in the terminal. Requests from the office's own machine (loopback) connect freely with no token. Every other HTTP request and WebSocket connection must carry the token as `?t=<token>`; a missing or wrong token is refused.

To connect another browser on your network, use the printed link or scan the terminal's QR code: that is the join URL, token included. The token changes on every start, so after a restart, scan the new code. To keep the office to this machine only, start with `--host 127.0.0.1`: there is then no join URL at all.

A paired phone (see [Droid Office for Android](#droid-office-for-android)) carries a **device token** instead. It opens the office the way the LAN token does, for every page, API and the WebSocket, but it keeps working across restarts until you forget the phone. The phone sends it as `Authorization: Bearer <token>` (or `?d=<token>`). The office keeps only its SHA-256, in its own `.droid-office/devices.json`. A device token that was forgotten is refused everywhere, even on loopback, so the phone knows to pair again.

An office that ran before this had a shared password and per-person accounts. Those are gone: the old `accounts.json` is left unread on disk (in the office's `.droid-office/`, next to `config.json`), and so is the old `chat.jsonl`. Delete them yourself if you want them gone; the office never reads, writes or serves them.

```
droid-office [dir] [options]

      --home <dir>        Where the office keeps its data without a [dir] (default ~/droid-office)
      --projects <dir>    Where the office looks for existing git checkouts to add as floors (default a
                          code folder in your home folder, else the home folder; also settable from ⚙️ Settings → **Building**)
  -p, --port <n>          Port (default 4600, env PORT)
  -H, --host <addr>       Bind address (default 0.0.0.0; 127.0.0.1 keeps the office on this machine)
      --agent <cmd>       The Droid command (default "droid", env DROID_OFFICE_AGENT)
      --agent-args <str>  Extra args for Droid, e.g. "--auto medium"
      --tls-cert <file>   Serve HTTPS with this cert…
      --tls-key <file>    …and key
      --self-signed       Serve HTTPS with a generated self-signed cert
      --trust-proxy       Trust X-Forwarded-* (behind Caddy/nginx)
      --max-workers <n>   Run at most n workers at once, across every floor (env DROID_OFFICE_MAX_WORKERS)
      --webhook <url>     Post to this Slack / Discord webhook when a worker needs input or finishes
      --city <name>       Put the office in a real city: its sun and live weather (open-meteo.com)
      --weather <kind>    Pin the weather: clear, cloudy, rain, storm, snow or fog

droid-office setup [--projects <dir>] [--project <dir>]... [--home <dir>]

  The first-start walkthrough again: the workspace folder, and which of the
  git checkouts in it are floors. With --projects / --project it asks nothing.
  Run it while the office is stopped.

droid-office prune [dir] [-n|--dry-run] [-f|--force]

  Removes leftover worker worktrees under .droid-office/worktrees/ and their
  office/* branches, in one floor's checkout (dir). Anything with uncommitted changes or unpushed commits is
  kept unless --force is given. A worker across several projects has worktrees of them in its
  own floor's workspace: prune each project to clear those out.
```

## Droid Office for Android

**Droid Office for Android** is a companion app for your phone: it lists the workers on each floor, hires new ones at any free desk, shows their live terminals and lets you prompt them or type into them. It talks to the same office over the same WebSocket as the browser. How to build and install it is in [`android/README.md`](android/README.md).

**App updates.** The app quietly checks the latest public GitHub release when you open it, and tells you when a newer Android app is available. Under **Offices → About**, or on the welcome screen before pairing, **Update app → Update now** downloads the APK with progress and opens Android's installer. Allow installs from Droid Office the first time, confirm the update and reopen the app. Your paired offices stay put. You can cancel the download or keep using the app while it runs. **Download newest version** opens the latest release in a browser if in-app updating doesn't work.

**Terminal views.** The phone runs a real terminal emulator, so a worker's terminal keeps its scrollback: swipe to read earlier lines, and **Latest** goes back to the bottom. In a full-screen program a swipe scrolls the program instead. There are two views. **Desktop**, the default, shows the terminal at your desktop's size, scaled to fit the phone, and never resizes it. **Phone** in the quick keys changes the PTY's columns and rows to fit readable text on the phone (the keyboard changes only the rows), so programs wrap and redraw at that size, and desktop views see the same grid. Tap **Phone** again or leave the terminal, and the size from before comes back. If a desktop window resizes the terminal meanwhile, the phone switches to Desktop and leaves that size alone. If the phone drops off while in Phone (out of range, the app closed), the office puts the size back itself.

**Pairing.** Open ⚙️ Settings → **Phone** and choose **📱 Pair a phone** (also in the **☰** menu and the command palette). The window shows a QR code, the addresses it carries and the phones already paired. Scan the code with the app. A few seconds later the phone shows up under **Paired phones**, marked *Just paired*. The window only opens on the office's own machine (its browser on `localhost`, or the Mac app): a phone or another browser on the LAN can't read the code or the list. The app also pairs from the join URL's QR code in the office's terminal; the terminal lists the Tailscale join URLs under it.

The QR code holds a link like this, every value URL-encoded:

```
droidoffice://pair?v=1&name=<this machine's name>&t=<this start's LAN token>&u=http://192.168.1.20:4600&u=http://my-mac.tailnet-name.ts.net:4600&u=http://100.101.102.103:4600
```

The app sends `POST /api/mobile/pair?t=<token>` with `{"device": "Pixel 10 Pro XL"}` to the first address that answers, and gets back `{"deviceId", "token", "office": {"name", "version"}}`. From then on it uses that device token, not the LAN token, so it stays paired when the office restarts and the LAN token changes. `GET /api/mobile/hello` checks an address and a token quickly. Hiring from the phone sends `worker.spawn` with `deskId: "auto"`, which seats the worker at the first free desk on the floor (then the first free bean bag), the same order the task queue uses.

**Wi-Fi and Tailscale.** The code lists the office's Wi-Fi (LAN) addresses first, then its Tailscale ones, labelled in the window: the MagicDNS name (`my-mac.tailnet-name.ts.net`) and the `100.x.y.z` address, read from `tailscale status --json` (the `tailscale` command on `PATH`, or the one inside `/Applications/Tailscale.app`). Wi-Fi works while the phone is on the same network as the office's machine. Tailscale works from anywhere once the phone is signed in to the same tailnet, with no ports opened to the internet. Without Tailscale, or signed out, the code just has the Wi-Fi addresses. An office started with `--host 127.0.0.1` can't be reached by a phone at all, and the window says so instead of showing a code. With `--self-signed` or `--tls-cert`, the addresses are `https://`.

**Forgetting a phone.** **Forget** next to a phone in the Phone window (click it twice to be sure) unpairs it: its token stops working at once, and any connection it has open is closed. Unpairing in the app does the same from the phone (`DELETE /api/mobile/pair` with its token). Lost a phone? Forget it here. Pair it again any time by scanning the code.

**Device tokens.** Each pairing mints a random 256-bit token, sent to the phone once. The office writes only its SHA-256, the phone's name and when it paired and was last seen to `devices.json` in the office's own `.droid-office/` (next to `subagents.json`), with mode 0600, and it compares tokens in constant time. *Last seen* is written at most once a minute. Up to 32 phones can be paired at once. Like the LAN token, a device token is as good as a shell on the office's machine: anyone holding one can drive Droid there.

## Choosing a model

Every worker, board agent and meeting seat is a Droid session: the office runs the [Droid CLI](https://docs.factory.ai/cli/getting-started/quickstart) (`--agent`, default `droid`; `DROID_OFFICE_AGENT` changes it), passing each one its prompt and resuming a saved session with `droid --resume <session-id>`. `--agent-args` adds extra flags for Droid, e.g. `--agent-args "--auto medium"`.

The hire, hand-off, queue, meeting and Subagents windows list every model droid can run, the same list as droid's own model picker: the office asks `droid exec` for it in JSON-RPC mode (`droid.list_models`, which starts no session and calls no model) and caches it for a minute, falling back to the models your own `~/.factory/settings.json` names (every custom model, plus your session default and favorites) when droid can't answer. They're grouped into your own models, Factory's and legacy ones, disabled models are left out, and the effort list (low, medium, high, xhigh or max) offers only the efforts the picked model takes, its own default named on **Default**. Leave the model on **Default** to run the office's Default worker (below), or whatever your Droid settings say when none is set; the option names the model and effort it runs, such as **Default (Opus 5.5)**. A pick is pinned for that worker only: interactive droid has no `--model` flag, so the office writes the model and effort into that worker's own settings overlay (`.droid-office/droid-<id>.json`), merged for that process alone. It stays with the worker across resumes and never changes your Factory settings.

Set the office's **Default worker** in ⚙️ Settings → **Workers**: a model and effort for whatever nobody picks one for: board agents, tasks the Queue agent adds, and meetings called without one. Until you set it, those start on `--agent` with Droid's own default model, as they always have. The hire, queue and meeting windows keep their own pickers and memory; a choice made there wins over the default.

The office generates `.droid-office/droid-hooks.json` and passes it as a per-process `--settings` overlay: lifecycle events report status to a token-protected loopback endpoint without editing your Factory settings or changing autonomy and approval policies. Complete login or any hook review in the worker's terminal if startup pauses. `/model` in a worker's terminal changes its model mid-session, and the office reads the model and effort Droid's own session bookkeeping names, so the badge next to a worker shows what it really runs.

## The office's prompts

Everything the office tells a worker by itself can be rewritten in ⚙️ Settings → **Workers** → **Prompts**: what **Hand to a worker**, **Review**, **Fix up & merge**, **Fix conflicts & merge** and **Ask a worker…** send from the issue and pull request boards, what a meeting about an issue or a review panel starts with, the note the queue adds to a worktree task, the three board agents' briefs, every part the meeting room hands out, the brief written into a workspace when one task spans several repositories, and the instructions for the model that writes the signs over workers' heads. Each one lists its `{{placeholders}}` (the issue number, the PR's branch, the file a meeting waits for, and so on), which the office fills in when it sends it, and warns when one the office counts on is missing. On a GitLab floor the same prompt reads in GitLab's words: `{{site}}`, `{{cli}}`, `{{pullName}}`, `{{ref}}`, `{{read}}`, `{{checkout}}`, `{{merge}}` and the rest become GitLab, `glab`, merge request, `!12` and the `glab mr` commands, so the office's own wording is word for word what it sends on each forge. **Default** puts the office's own wording back. The Jira board's hand-off prompt isn't one of them. They're the same on every floor and kept in the office's `.droid-office/prompts.json`, with the Default worker.

## One command on AWS

If you have the AWS CLI logged in, one command gives you your own office on EC2. No Terraform needed:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/nikships/droid-office && cd droid-office
deploy/aws.sh up
```

After that, the whole lifecycle is four more commands:

```bash
deploy/aws.sh open      # tunnel to the office and open it in your browser
deploy/aws.sh pause     # stop the machine to save money (asks first); only the disk and IP are billed
deploy/aws.sh resume    # start it again: same address, same files, then open it
deploy/aws.sh destroy   # delete the machine, disk, IP, security group and key pair (asks first)
```

What `up` does, in about 2 minutes:

1. Creates an SSH key pair (kept in `~/.config/droid-office/aws/<name>/`).
2. Creates a security group that opens **only SSH (port 22), and only to your current IP**. The office itself is never on the internet.
3. Gives the machine a fixed Elastic IP and launches a **t3.xlarge** (4 vCPU, 16 GiB) Ubuntu 24.04 instance with a 50 GiB disk.
4. Installs Node 22, git, the GitHub CLI and the **Droid CLI**. It clones the latest droid-office from GitHub and runs `npm i`. The office keeps its data in `~/droid-office` on the machine and clones projects into `~/workspace/<owner>/<repo>`.
5. Runs the office under systemd with `Restart=always`, so it comes back after a crash or a reboot, and `KillMode=process`, so restarting it leaves the workers running. It listens on `127.0.0.1:4600` on the machine, so the only way in is an SSH tunnel.
6. Opens an SSH tunnel and your browser at `http://localhost:4600`. The tunnel lands on the machine's loopback, which the office trusts, so you walk straight in: no login, no token, nothing to write down.
7. The office opens on its elevator with no floors yet. It lists every repository your GitHub token can see: pick one and it becomes the first floor.

Everything goes through SSH, so there are no certificate warnings. Keep the terminal open while you use the office; Ctrl-C closes the tunnel. Next time, run `deploy/aws.sh open`. If port 4600 is taken on your machine, it picks the next free one.

**Reviewing what workers build.** Every web server a worker runs is listed on the **🌐 Services** board. Run `deploy/aws.sh service 5173` for the one on port 5173: it opens http://localhost:5173 on your computer. The tunnel ends at the office's own port, and the office relays it to the worker's server on 5173, so nothing new is opened on the machine. Keep one terminal per service open while you look.

If your IP keeps changing, `deploy/aws.sh allow anywhere` opens SSH to every IP. That's a reasonable trade: SSH only accepts your key, and the office stays behind the tunnel.

```bash
deploy/aws.sh open                 # tunnel + open the office in your browser
deploy/aws.sh service 5173         # open a worker's web server from the 🌐 Services board
deploy/aws.sh allow 203.0.113.7    # let an IP reach SSH (CIDR ok; "me", "anywhere")
deploy/aws.sh revoke 203.0.113.7   # …and take it back
deploy/aws.sh status               # instance, address, office up?, allowed IPs
deploy/aws.sh resize t3.2xlarge    # bigger or smaller machine; same address, ~1-2 min of downtime
deploy/aws.sh update               # install the latest droid-office and restart
deploy/aws.sh ssh | logs           # get on the box / follow the office logs
```

**Upgrading from the office.** **⬆️ Upgrade the office** in the **☰** menu checks GitHub for new commits on the branch the office was installed from. When there are any, an orange **⬆️ Update** button shows up on the top bar, and it lists them. **Upgrade now** builds the new version next to the running one. Meanwhile the office keeps working and a banner says so. A failed build changes nothing. Once the build succeeds, the office swaps it in and restarts, and you get an *Upgrading the office* dialog, which you can close. The page still reloads onto the new version a few seconds after the office comes back. Workers keep working through it: their terminals run in a process of their own, which the new version picks back up. (An office set up before this lets them keep running from its next upgrade on; the first one resumes them with *continue*. One upgrade that does restart the terminals is the move to the Droid-only office: its terminal-host protocol changed, so every worker's terminal is ended once by the new version and each worker is resumed where it left off.)

An office created before the SSH tunnel served HTTPS on port 443 with a self-signed certificate. Run `deploy/aws.sh up` once to move it over: 443 closes and the office moves behind the tunnel.

**Machines from before.** Offices set up before this release had teammate SSH users, extra keys and a restricted tunnel user. Source changes never touch running hosts, so audit an old machine yourself before removing anything: check `~/.ssh/authorized_keys` and any `office` user, the sudoers entries and the SSH config the old provisioner wrote, confirm nothing you still use tunnels through them, and only then remove the keys and users you no longer want.

Useful options for `up`:

- `--project owner/repo` also clones that repo as the office's first floor. Without it, you pick projects in the elevator. (Before, the office was started in the GitHub origin of the directory you ran `up` from, which is usually droid-office itself. An office set up that way keeps its data in that checkout after `up`, and you can take droid-office off the building in the elevator.)
- `--instance-type`, `--disk` and `--region` set the machine size, disk size and region.
- `--allow <ip>` lets more IPs reach SSH from the start.
- `--name <name>` runs several offices side by side.

**Droid sign-in.** Workers run Droid on the machine, so it has to be signed in there. You can do this either way:

- Pass `--factory-api-key <key>`, which writes it to `/etc/droid-office/env` for Droid's sessions.
- Do nothing, and the first worker waits for sign-in. Get on the box with `deploy/aws.sh ssh` (or open the worker's terminal in the office), run `droid`, and log in.

**GitHub.** By default, your local `gh auth token` is used to sign in the GitHub CLI on the machine. It's needed for private repos, the issue and PR boards, and for workers to push branches and open PRs. Anyone who reaches the office can use that token, so pass `--github-token <fine-grained token>` or `--no-github-token` if that's too much.

## Running it on a VPS

The simplest private setup needs no certificates at all. Run `droid-office --host 127.0.0.1` and connect with `ssh -L 4600:localhost:4600 you@server`, then open http://localhost:4600. The tunnel lands on loopback, so you walk straight in with no login.

To serve it on a real domain instead, put the office behind HTTPS. The simplest setup is Caddy, which gets certificates automatically:

```caddy
# /etc/caddy/Caddyfile
office.example.com {
    reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:4600
}
```

```bash
droid-office setup --projects ~/workspace --project owner/repo   # once; or pick projects in the office
droid-office --host 127.0.0.1 --trust-proxy
```

Caddy proxies WebSockets out of the box. With nginx, forward the Host and Upgrade headers:

```nginx
location / {
    proxy_pass http://127.0.0.1:4600;
    proxy_http_version 1.1;
    proxy_set_header Host $host;
    proxy_set_header Upgrade $http_upgrade;
    proxy_set_header Connection "upgrade";
    proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-For $proxy_add_x_forwarded_for;
    proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-Proto $scheme;
    proxy_read_timeout 1d;
}
```

To keep the office running, use a systemd unit:

```ini
# /etc/systemd/system/droid-office.service
[Unit]
Description=Droid Office
After=network.target

[Service]
User=dev
WorkingDirectory=/home/dev
ExecStart=/usr/bin/env droid-office --host 127.0.0.1 --trust-proxy
Restart=on-failure
# Restarting the office leaves the workers' terminals running for the next one to pick up.
KillMode=process

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target
```

If you don't have a domain, `--self-signed` serves HTTPS directly. Browsers will warn once before trusting it.

## How it works

```
browser ──HTTPS/WSS──▶ droid-office (Node)
                         ├─ node-pty ─▶ droid (one PTY per worker)
                         │    └─ headless xterm mirror ─▶ laptop screen frames + late-join snapshots
                         ├─ loopback-only hook server ◀── Droid hooks (per-worker token)
                         ├─ gh issue/pr list (cached, refreshed every 90s)
                         ├─ port scan every 4s ─▶ 🌐 Services board; relay for service tunnels
                         ├─ ps + lsof every 4s ─▶ guests: agents run by hand in a floor's checkout
                         └─ /api/image ─▶ fetches pictures for the walls (cached)
```

- **Android app updates.** The app's `UpdateController` checks the public GitHub latest-release API on foreground entry, at most hourly per running process (15 minutes after failures), or immediately on a manual check. The tag's commit count is Android's version code. `update/GitHubUpdates.kt` uses a separate unauthenticated HTTPS client with HTTP cache validators; downloads start only on **Update now**, stream to private cache and are checked against GitHub's SHA-256 digest and the installed package/signing key before a narrow, read-only `FileProvider` URI opens Android's installer. Failed checks don't block the office, finished APKs are reverified and reused, and the latest-release webpage is always offered as the fallback when a newer version is known. See [android/docs/updates.md](android/docs/updates.md).
- **Status.** Droid workers start with a `--settings .droid-office/droid-hooks.json` overlay: hooks (`SessionStart`, `UserPromptSubmit`, `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `Notification`, `Stop`) post to `/hooks/droid`, a server bound to `127.0.0.1` and authenticated by a random per-worker token. The hooks use `curl` when it's there and fall back to a small Node helper when it isn't; they merge for that process only and never edit your Factory settings. A worker's first message (its task, a board agent's or subagent's brief, the carry-on after a restart) is not passed on droid's command line, because droid started with a prompt argument never opens `/rewind-conversation` in that run: the office pastes it into the input box when `SessionStart` says droid can take input, or, when no `SessionStart` has come 15 seconds after the start, once the screen shows the `? for help` footer. A `Notification` with `permission_prompt` or `elicitation_dialog` means *needs input*, and one with `idle_prompt` (what droid sends when a turn is cancelled with Esc) means *done*, as does `Stop`. A `PreToolUse` of `AskUser` (or `AskUserQuestion`, `request_user_input`) also means *needs input*. The prompt is answered when the next tool starts or when the tool it asked about finishes (`PostToolUse`); droid sends nothing in between. A subagent droid starts with the Task tool posts with the worker's token under its own session, announced by a `SessionStart` with `calling_session_id`: its permission prompts and questions count for the worker, while its own start, prompt and `Stop` don't, and the worker keeps (and resumes) its own session. Some turns start with no hook (a message queued with Ctrl+Enter), and hooks that fire while the office is down for longer than they retry are lost, so every second the office (`reconcile` in `src/server/workers.ts`, the patterns in `src/server/droid-screen.ts`) also reads the bottom rows of each agent's terminal: droid's spinner line (`Press ESC to stop`), a permission prompt or AskUser dialog, or the `? for help` footer at rest. Once the screen has said one thing for 4 seconds with no hook in that time, a status it contradicts follows it: a busy screen that is still printing means *working*, a dialog means *needs input*, and a footer with nothing printed for 4 seconds means *done*.
- **Shells.** Press **B** at an empty desk to open a plain login shell for dev servers, git or tests. It subscribes and streams like any worker's terminal.
- **Guests.** Every 4 seconds one scan for the whole building (`GuestScanner` in `src/server/guests.ts`) lists this user's processes with `ps` (pid, parent, terminal, start time and executable; the command line only for `--resume <id>` and the office's own `--settings …/.droid-office/droid-….json`), keeps the agent CLIs (`droid`, `claude`, `codex`, `opencode`, `grok`, `muse`) that have a terminal, and reads each one's working directory with `lsof -a -p <pids> -d cwd -Fn` (`/proc/<pid>/cwd` on Linux; nothing on Windows). Left out: anything under a `droid-office-ptys` terminal host, the office itself or one of its workers' terminals, anything launched with an office's hook settings (another office's workers too), an agent under another agent (its helpers), and headless `exec` runs. The working directory, symlinks resolved, is matched against the floors in `floors.json`: the floor whose checkout it is, else the innermost floor it's inside (a subfolder, or a worktree under `.droid-office/worktrees/`); the home floor matches only the home folder itself, and a folder that isn't in any floor's checkout shows nowhere. Inside an office worker's own worktree or workspace, the process is listed on that worker (`WorkerInfo.outside`) rather than seated. Each guest is a `WorkerInfo` with `guest` set (pid, tty, provider, cwd, start time and `seen`), sent with the same `worker.update` and `worker.remove` messages as workers but kept by the floor's `Guests`, never the `WorkerManager`: so the worker limit, the queue, the team, meetings, sending home and the leave-on-merge sweep never see it, and the server refuses every message that acts on a worker (prompt, kill, resume, shoot, PR, changes) for a guest id with a toast saying why. Its id is its pid and start time, so a later process with the same pid is someone new, and `guests.json` in the floor's `.droid-office/` keeps its desk, name and whether its last turn was seen, so it's back where it was after an office restart for as long as its process runs. Hires, the queue and subagents treat its seat and name as taken. A droid guest's session is the one in `~/.factory/sessions/<folder>/` it was started to `--resume`, or, when it's the only droid guest without one in that folder, the transcript there written most recently since it started (never an office worker's); with two in one folder nobody can say whose transcript is whose, so neither gets one. Its status is read from the end of that transcript: an `agent_turn_outcome` line ends a turn (*done*, waiting on you until someone opens its window, unless it had finished before the office first saw it), a prompt or a reply since then is *working*, and nothing yet is *ready*; the model comes from the session's `.settings.json`. Without a transcript it is only *running*. Opening its window gets a banner from the server instead of a terminal snapshot, redrawn when the guest changes; the window takes no input. A guest whose process is missing from two scans in a row goes home. Bringing a guest in (`guest.bringIn`, `Floor.bringIn`) is the one time the office touches a process it didn't start, and only when the owner asks. The guest must be droid, working in the floor's checkout itself (an office worker runs there or in a worktree of its own), with a session tied to it, and between turns: `GuestInfo.cantBringIn` says which of these fails, and its transcript is read once more right before, so a turn started since the last scan isn't cut off (a permission prompt reads as mid-turn, so it is refused too). It needs room under the worker limit. Its desk stays held while `stopGuest` checks with `ps` that the pid still has the same start time and sends it SIGTERM; droid saves its session and gives its terminal back to the shell. Once `ps` no longer shows it (within 5 seconds, else the guest stays a guest and the owner is told), the guest leaves and `WorkerManager.takeIn` seats a worker at its desk, with its name and color, in the checkout, running `droid --resume <session>` on the session's own model. Only then: two droids in one session would write over each other. A scan from before it quit doesn't seat it again. `droid-office prune` runs the same discovery and keeps any worktree an agent is running in, even with `--force`.
- **Nearby offices.** After its HTTP or HTTPS server starts, the CLI uses `bonjour-service` to advertise `_droidoffice._tcp.local` over mDNS. The instance name identifies the laptop and port; the SRV record uses the actual listening port and only addresses served by the bind. Wildcard binds advertise LAN adapters, with IPv4 first, and exclude common VPN and container adapters. TXT contains just `v=1` and `scheme=http` or `scheme=https`, never a password, token, project path or worker data. Discovery is best-effort and stays on the local network. SIGINT and SIGTERM unpublish the service and close its multicast socket without changing how workers shut down or survive a restart.
- **Isolated branches.** When you hire with a task, you can tick *own git worktree*. The worker then gets its own `office/<name>` branch under `.droid-office/worktrees/`, so parallel workers never share a checkout. Just before cutting it (a hire, a queue task, a meeting), the office runs `git fetch --no-tags origin <branch>` (`src/server/worktrees.ts`, 15s, a burst of hires shares one fetch, and a failure is logged once) and branches from `origin/<branch>` when the checkout is strictly behind it, so pull requests merged since you last pulled are in it. It branches from the local HEAD instead when that already has all of origin (commits not pushed yet), or when there's no origin or the fetch fails. When both have moved on, it starts from origin and a toast says how many local commits it left out (`starts from origin/main, without the N commits on main that origin doesn't have`). The floor's own checkout is never moved or cloned; the fetch only refreshes the base a worktree is cut from. The office keeps `worktree.branch` on the branch the worktree is actually on: it reads it when the worker comes to rest or its process exits, when the office starts, when the pull-request list arrives, and before **O** or sending the worker home. A branch the worker creates itself (`git checkout -b`), or a rename of the office branch (`git branch -m`, matched only by the reflog line `Branch: renamed refs/heads/…`), is the one that is followed, and `worktree.made` remembers `office/<name>` until that rename. Deleting the office branch is not a rename, so send-home then removes only the worktree and leaves the branch the worker is on. Sending it home deletes the branch the worker made. The office branch is deleted only when it holds nothing that branch lacks, and is kept while it still has commits no remote, the project's checkout, or the current branch has — those commits still count as work after the worker has switched away. A branch that already existed when the worker was hired (its reflog is older, like `main`) is never deleted. When you send that worker home you choose whether to keep the worktree and branch, delete the worktree, or delete both, and the office warns you first about uncommitted changes or commits no remote has. `droid-office prune` clears out whatever was kept, once it is safe, and treats `worktree.made` as that worker's branch too. With *Workers whose pull request merged* set to go home by themselves (`src/server/leave-on-merge.ts`, saved in `.droid-office/leave-on-merge.json`), each floor looks again whenever the PR list comes back, a queue task's PR is linked or a worker changes, and uses the same call as the purple bubble (`workerPr` in `src/shared/status.ts`). A worker goes with the office's usual check for work its worktree would lose, except that commits up to the merged PR's head (GitHub's `headRefOid`, GitLab's `diffHeadSha`) count as delivered, even once the forge has deleted the branch.
- **Pull requests.** **O** at a worktree worker's desk runs `git push -u origin <branch>` in its worktree, then `gh pr create` (or the floor's forge) with the task as the body and the commits listed. `<branch>` is the one the worktree is on now, which may be one the worker made itself rather than `office/<name>`. The base is the branch the office was on when the worktree was cut, if that branch is on the remote. The PR number is saved with the worker, so the board can point back at the desk.
- **Across repositories.** A worker hired with other floors' projects (`WorkerInfo.repos`, `makeWorkspace` in `src/server/workers.ts`) gets a workspace folder, `.droid-office/worktrees/<name>/` on its own floor, with a worktree of its own project and of each other one in it, all on `office/<name>`, named after their checkouts. Each is cut from that checkout's fetched base (the same `git fetch origin <branch>` as a single worktree; the floor's checkout is never cloned or moved), and its `from` branch is what that project's pull request targets. Two checkouts of the same repository are refused, and so are more than 8 other projects. The workspace isn't a repository; the agent starts there with `GIT_CEILING_DIRECTORIES` set to its parent, so git run in it finds nothing instead of the floor's own checkout, and the `worker.repos` prompt is written there as `AGENTS.md`. If one project can't have its worktree, the ones already made are removed again. **O** pushes and opens a pull request in each worktree that has commits and no pull request yet (the issue's `Closes #n` only in the home project's; the others say `Part of owner/repo#n`), then puts a list of all of them between `<!-- agent-office:related -->` markers in each description, replacing the list on later presses. A worker across repositories goes home by itself once no pull request of its is open on any floor's board, one has merged, and none of its worktrees holds work those merges didn't deliver; a failed check keeps the worker. `droid-office prune` leaves a workspace alone while worktrees are in it, and in the other projects lists the worker's branch as checked out elsewhere (and prunes it once that office no longer lists the worker).
- **Changes.** While a desk's Changes window is open, the office runs `git status` and `git diff` in that worker's checkout every two seconds and pushes the file list when it differs. A worker across repositories has a tab per repository, each diffed against that floor's own branch. The diff is against the merge base with the newer of the branch the office was opened on and its origin copy, so it covers the worker's commits and its uncommitted edits, and a pull request merged on the forge that the project never pulled is not shown as the worker's change. Commit is `git add -A && git commit`, discard is `git restore` / `git clean` (or `reset --hard` for everything), and Open PR pushes the branch and runs `gh pr create`.
- **Terminals.** Each worker's PTY lives in `droid-office-ptys` (`src/server/ptyhost.ts`), a detached process the server starts for each floor and talks to over a Unix socket in that project's `.droid-office/`. On SIGTERM the server leaves the PTYs running there, and the next server takes them back with a snapshot of each screen. Hooks that fire in between retry until it's up. If no server comes back within 30 minutes, the PTYs are ended. The server mirrors each PTY in a headless xterm. Each window that opens the terminal gets a serialized snapshot, then the live stream; closing one window detaches only it. Laptops get compact per-row diffs a few times a second. The PTY takes the size of whichever window typed into it last. Android draws the snapshot and stream in xterm.js inside a WebView, so it keeps the snapshot's scrollback. Its **Desktop** view never sends `term.resize`. Its **Phone** view sends one when selected or when its viewport changes, measured at readable text size; switching it off or leaving the terminal sends back the size from before, unless another window resized it since, in which case the phone switches to Desktop. The server also remembers, per phone connection, the size each terminal had before that phone's first resize, and when the connection closes it puts back every terminal still at the size the phone last set.
- **Scrollback and search.** Every 15 seconds, for each worker that printed something new, and again as the office shuts down, its last 3,000 lines are serialized, colors included, to `.droid-office/scrollback/<worker>.ansi`. A worker that starts again (after a restart, or when you resume it) gets its old lines replayed into its terminal first, then a dim *office restarted* line. `GET /api/search?q=` matches case-insensitively, treating runs of whitespace as one space, over each worker's terminal buffer. It returns the newest lines first, each distinct line once. The browser finds the hit again in its own copy of the terminal and scrolls to it. An office from before this release may still have a `chat.jsonl` in its `.droid-office/`; it is left on disk and never read.
- **Pictures in prompts.** `src/client/ui/images.ts` uploads each pasted or dropped picture as it's added (`POST /api/prompt/image`, which checks the file's first bytes, not the browser's word for it, and keeps it in `drops/staged/<id>/`; `DELETE` takes it out). A message that carries a prompt (`worker.spawn`, `worker.prompt`, `station.prompt`, `queue.add`, `meeting.start`) lists the picture ids in `images`, and the server turns them into paths: `DropStore.adopt` copies each into the worker's own drops and `withImages` (`src/shared/drops.ts`) appends the numbered list to the prompt. The ids stay staged until the message has been handled (a meeting adopts them once per worker), and a queue task holds them in `queue.json` until it starts. Closing a dialog without sending deletes its staged pictures.
- **Sky.** The server decides the weather and tells every window (`src/server/sky.ts`), and every browser renders it as a night sky. Without `--city`, the office sits in the middle of the host's time zone at 40° north (34° south if its clocks go forward in January), and the weather changes every 20 to 50 minutes, with the season's odds. With `--city` (a name, or `lat,lon`), it asks open-meteo.com for the place once and for its current weather every 15 minutes; no key is needed, and nothing but the city's name and coordinates is sent. The office has no roof, so the moon and the sky light the rooms too, but only faintly. A few lines added to every lit material put a faint neon fill in the office and the garage, pools of light around each open laptop's screen (the nearest 16 to you; the main light in the room) and pools around the lamps outside (`src/client/world/sky.ts`). The wall boards are unlit and dimmed to `BOARD_GLOW` in `main.ts`. A server on AWS keeps UTC, so on an office deployed with `deploy/aws.sh`, put `DROID_OFFICE_CITY="Portland, Oregon"` in `/etc/droid-office/env` and restart the service.
- **Services.** Every 4 seconds the office lists the TCP ports its user's processes listen on (`ss`, or `lsof` on macOS). It credits each port to the worker whose terminal started it. It goes by the process tree first. For a server that detached from it, it uses the `DROID_OFFICE_WORKER_ID` the process inherited (Linux), then whether it runs inside that worker's worktree. Ports that answer HTTP are shown. A request for `localhost:<port>` that reaches the office's own port (that's what a service tunnel does) is relayed to that server, WebSockets included, so hot reload works.
- **Pictures.** WebGL can only draw an image from another site if that site sends CORS headers, and most don't. So the office fetches each picture itself (`/api/image`, images up to 15 MB) and serves it from its own origin. Any image link works, and a picture on a worker's dev server does too. Browsers shrink each one to 1024 px before it goes on the wall.
- **Board agents.** Each kiosk is a seat of its own (`station-issues`, `station-pulls`, `station-queue`) that the desks, the bean bags and the queue never use, and its agent doesn't count against the queue's *workers at once*. Its first prompt starts with a brief (`src/server/stations.ts`) saying what it's for. The agents reach the queue with `office-queue` (`bin/office-queue.js`, plain Node), which the office writes into the floor's `.droid-office/bin/` and puts first on their `PATH` whichever agent runs them: `office-queue list` shows each task's id, status, title, worker and PR, `office-queue add --title "…" [--issue 12]` queues a task with its prompt on stdin (or `--prompt`) and prints its id, and `office-queue remove <id>` takes a waiting one off. Under the hood that's `GET`, `POST` (`{title, prompt, issue?}`) or `DELETE ?task=` on `$DROID_OFFICE_HOOK_URL/office/queue?worker=$DROID_OFFICE_WORKER_ID` with `Authorization: Bearer $DROID_OFFICE_HOOK_TOKEN`, all three set in their environment, over the office's loopback hook port. Only a board agent's own token is accepted; anyone else gets a 401 or 403, which the command prints. The queue agent is told to only ever add tasks.
- **Subagents.** A subagent is an ordinary worker whose `WorkerInfo.lead` names the worker that hired it; `workers.json` keeps it across restarts, and a lead that doesn't come back (or is a subagent itself) leads nobody. Leads hire with `office-workers` (`bin/office-workers.js`, plain Node), which the office writes into the floor's `.droid-office/bin/team/` and puts on the `PATH` of every agent, at desks and kiosks alike (shells don't get it). Every command is a `POST` of `{action, …}` to `$DROID_OFFICE_HOOK_URL/office/workers?worker=$DROID_OFFICE_WORKER_ID` with `Authorization: Bearer $DROID_OFFICE_HOOK_TOKEN`, over the loopback hook port, and the token says who's asking, so a worker only ever sees and runs its own team. The floor's `Team` (`src/server/team.ts`) answers it: `whoami`, `list`, `hire`, `events` (what `office-workers wait` polls for), `read` (the end of a subagent's terminal, as text), `send` (a prompt, or a resume for one that's asleep), `dismiss` (sending home; its worktree and branch go only when they hold no work) and, for a subagent, `report`. A hire is refused when subagents are off, for a subagent, a shell, a meeting seat or another kiosk's agent, for a desk worker when only the Team lead may hire, and past the per-lead limit; the office's worker limit applies as to any hire. The subagent sits at the free desk nearest its lead's (`nearestFreeSeat` in `src/shared/layout.ts`), else the first free bean bag, on what its lead asked for, else the Subagents setting, else the Default worker. A worktree is cut after the same `git fetch` as a queue task's. Its first prompt is the `subagent.brief` (its lead, where it works, how to report) followed by its lead's brief, and the `--title` goes on its card. The team keeps what each lead hasn't heard yet in `.droid-office/team.json` (400 events per floor): reports, questions, and a subagent finishing a turn without reporting, needing input, stopping or leaving. Four seconds after news, a lead that's done or idle, and that nobody typed into in the last five seconds, gets the `subagent.nudge` prompt with one line per subagent; a busy lead is told once it comes to rest. The Team lead stands at `station-lead`, a kiosk on the east wall that the desks and the queue never use, with the `station.lead` brief. The settings (`src/server/subagents.ts`) are the building's, in the office's `.droid-office/subagents.json`. While subagents and the skill are both on, the office writes `~/.factory/skills/droid-office-subagents/SKILL.md` at every start and whenever the settings change, and removes it when either is off. The file carries a line saying the office wrote it: a `SKILL.md` there without that line is the user's own and is never touched. The skill tells droid to use it only when `DROID_OFFICE_WORKER_ID` is set and `office-workers whoami` answers, so it does nothing in droid sessions outside the office, and it sends the agent to `office-workers help` for the commands.
- **Jira.** The office's connection (`src/server/jira.ts`) is one Jira Cloud account for the whole building, kept in the office's `.droid-office/jira.json`; browsers get the site, the email and the account's name, never the token. Every request goes through Atlassian's API gateway (`https://api.atlassian.com/ex/jira/<cloudId>`, the cloud id read from the site's public `/_edge/tenant_info`), the one place a scoped read-only token works, and a classic token too. Connecting runs a small search to check the token, since a `read:jira-work` token isn't allowed to ask who it is. A floor's epic is checked to exist and be an epic, and saved in the floor's `jira-epic.json`. The Jira tab is every direct child of the epic (`parent = KEY ORDER BY Rank ASC`, through `/rest/api/3/search/jql`, up to 500), sorted into *To Do*, *In Progress* and *Done* by Jira's status category. It's read on the same clock as the GitHub boards: every 90 seconds while you're on the floor or a worker is busy, every 10 minutes otherwise, and it waits out the `Retry-After` when Jira rate limits it. Each request names a language (the office machine's, then English) in `Accept-Language`, since Jira names issue types and statuses in an arbitrary one when asked for any. Descriptions and comments are Atlassian Document Format, which the office turns into markdown. The office only sends GET requests and searches.
- **Factory.** The office's connection (`src/server/factory/`, [docs/factory.md](docs/factory.md)) is one Factory API key for the whole building, kept in the office's `.droid-office/factory.json`; browsers get its fingerprint, whose it is and what it reaches, never the key. Connecting sends one cheap GET per group to `https://api.factory.ai/api/v0`, all at once: `/computers/providers`, `/sessions?limit=1`, `/automations/ci/repository-owners` (which also says whether Factory's GitHub integration is connected), `/wiki`, `/organization/users` and `/service-accounts`. Whose key it is comes from `/computers` (the human owner of its computers) matched against the organization's users, else the only user. The answer goes back once all but the sessions call are in, since `/sessions` can take a minute on a cold call; that group reads *checking* until it lands. A key every group turns down with a 401 is refused. The office checks the saved key again at every start (each group reads *checking* until its own answer lands), and a 401 on any later call marks it rejected and stops every poll. Each Factory feature (`src/server/factory/<feature>.ts`) has a slice of one `FactoryState`, which every connection gets in `welcome` and in `factory` messages, a few times a second at most and only when something but a poll's time changed (or a minute has passed, so ages stay right). The registry (`registry.ts`) polls each one only while connected, never twice at once, at its own pace (computers every minute and each managed computer's metrics every 3, sessions every 90 seconds, CI and wiki every 5 minutes), faster while one of your windows or boards shows it (`factory.watch`) or something is in flight, and backs off after errors and 429s. Calls give up after 20 seconds (60 under `/sessions`) and a GET is tried once more. A feature's actions and on-demand reads are its routes under `/api/factory/<feature>/…`, behind the same gate as every `/api/*` route; anything but a GET must come from the office's own page or a paired phone.
- **Droid sessions and credits.** `src/server/factory/sessions.ts` reads the newest 50 sessions every 90 seconds (20 while the Sessions window is open or a session on a Factory computer runs), and the 50 before them every 10 minutes. A session's credits, and a status fresher than the list's, come only from reading it by itself (`GET /sessions/{id}`): the office does that for at most 24 sessions a poll, three at once, the running ones and its own workers' (every floor's, desk and cloud workers and guests alike) first, again when the list says the session changed, every 15 seconds while it runs and every minute while its worker works. Factory only gives a session's total, so the office keeps a ledger in its `.droid-office/factory-credits.json` of each total as it read it: what a total grew by since the last read is what that session spent that day, and a session seen for the first time has all its credits put on the day it was last active, which is an estimate for the days before the office started counting (the TV hatches those bars). It keeps a month. Disconnecting or another account's key deletes it. The transcript, messages, interrupts, settings, deleting and new sessions are the routes in [docs/factory.md](docs/factory.md).
- **CI automations.** `src/server/factory/ci.ts` reads Factory's GitHub owners, Droid workflow runs and workflow PRs (`/automations/ci/jobs`) every poll, and the workflow scan only once the scan's own cache (`cacheTtlMs`, 30 minutes) has run out, or on Rescan (`?forceRefresh=true`). `GET /api/factory/ci/repositories` lists where a workflow can go, kept 10 minutes. `POST /api/factory/ci/edit` checks the request, turns it into the body of Factory's undocumented `POST /automations/ci/edit` (`ciEditBody` in `src/shared/factory-ci.ts`, written up in [docs/factory.md](docs/factory.md#ci-automations)) and answers with the pull request Factory opened; for 3 minutes after, the feature polls every minute so the PR shows up. The board (`world/factory-ci.ts`) redraws only when what it shows changes, its ages to the minute.
- **Droid Computers.** The computers feature (`src/server/factory/computers.ts`, shapes in `src/shared/factory-computers.ts`) reads `GET /computers?includeProvisioningSteps=true` every minute, every 15 seconds while the compute wall is in view, the window is open, or a computer is provisioning or waking. Providers are read every 30 minutes and the computer secrets' names every 5 (the values never come back from Factory). Every 3 minutes, each managed computer's metrics are read from just after the last sample the office has (24 hours back the first time), and the last 6 hours are kept; a BYOM computer has no metrics. A managed computer that sent no sample in 20 minutes is *asleep*. After **Wake** resumes one, it reads *waking* and its metrics are read every minute until a sample comes, an error does, or 12 minutes pass. *This machine* is the computer whose name is the office machine's host name. Its routes (`/api/factory/computers/…`) are `POST` (make one), `POST /bulk`, `GET /repositories` (Factory's GitHub repositories, kept 10 minutes), `GET`/`PATCH /secrets`, and per computer `GET` (the latest, with steps), `GET /metrics?hours=` (1 to 96), `PATCH` (name, remote user), `DELETE` (only with its name as `confirm`), `POST /restart` (`resume` or `reboot`), `/refresh`, `/install-deps` and `/activity`. A change to a computer polls the list again at once; making, deleting, waking and rebooting one is announced with a toast. A secret's value goes straight to Factory and is never kept, logged or put in an error. The wall (`src/client/world/factory-computers.ts`) is two canvases in one bezel, this machine's and the fleet's, each redrawn only when what it shows changes, so the machine's updates don't re-upload the bigger one.
- **Cloud workers.** `src/server/factory/cloud.ts` is the `cloud` Factory feature; each floor keeps its own cloud workers in `src/server/cloud-workers.ts`, apart from the `WorkerManager`, so nothing there (PTYs, hooks, worktrees, the queue, the team, meetings, the worker limit) ever sees one. Hiring (`POST /api/factory/cloud/hire`) holds the desk, makes the session (`POST /sessions` with the computer, the folder and `sessionSettings`), seats the worker and sends the first prompt as its first message, its pictures inline as base64. `server.ts` hands every `worker.*`, `term.*` and `changes.*` message about a cloud worker to the feature: a prompt is a message to the session, attach and detach mark its window open, a kill interrupts it (and deletes the session when asked), and the rest are refused with a toast. While the office is connected the feature reads each working one's session every 4 seconds and each resting one's every minute, and the newest few messages only when the session moved. Running or pending is working; idle after a turn the office started, or one it saw run, is done (the same ding, notification and flag a local worker gets). Right after a message Factory can still say idle with the old message count, so a turn counts as over once the session was seen running, a reply made after the message went is listed, or 90 seconds went by. Factory lists a message many seconds after it was made, so a turn that ended before its reply was listed reads the newest messages for up to 90 seconds more, to put the reply at the desk. A 404, or its computer gone from the computers slice or in error, puts it in an error state with the reason. Factory 404s a session it has only just made for a few seconds, so a read 404s without harm until the office has read the session once or it is 3 minutes old, and a message, interrupt or delete for a session made in the last 30 seconds tries again a few times over about ten seconds (`src/server/factory/new-session.ts`, which the Sessions routes use too). Each one's turn is saved with it, so after a restart it's back at its desk and its next read picks the session up by id.
- **Task queue.** `queue.json` holds the tasks in order, each with the model and effort it was given. A task is seated when a desk is free and fewer than the limit of its own tasks are running (workers hired by hand, board agents and meetings don't count; the office's worker limit caps everyone). A worktree task is seated only after `git fetch origin <branch>` (and the queue tries again when that fetch finishes); it does not fetch when there is nowhere to sit. It finishes when its worker ends its turn (the Droid `Stop` hook), stops, or is sent home. The PR is matched by the forge's closing-issue references (`closes #12`) or by the worker's branch. When the worker moves onto a branch of its own, the queue rewrites `task.branch` so the pull request opened from there still matches.
- **Bookshelf.** `src/server/docs.ts` lists a floor's Markdown (`.md` and `.markdown`) with `git ls-files --cached --others --exclude-standard`. That covers what's committed plus new files that aren't ignored, so `node_modules`, build output and `.droid-office/` stay off the shelf. A folder that isn't a git checkout is walked instead, skipping hidden, dependency and build folders. Each doc's title is its front matter `title:` or its first heading, read from its first 4 KB, and the list is cached for a few seconds (`/api/docs`). Only Markdown can be read (`/api/docs/file`, up to 2 MB), and only pictures can be fetched for a doc (`/api/docs/picture`, up to 10 MB), both inside the checkout after following links. The browser renders the doc with the same sanitizer as issue and PR text. Links to files that aren't docs point at `…/blob/HEAD/` on GitHub or `…/-/blob/HEAD/` on GitLab, from the floor's `origin` remote. The book in your hands is the `reading` flag the office keeps on you while the window is open.
- **AutoWiki.** `src/server/factory/wiki.ts` ([docs/factory.md](docs/factory.md#autowiki)) keeps each floor's AutoWiki in the `wiki` slice: the newest run of the floor's repository (from `GET /wiki`, matched to its `origin` remote) and its history (`GET /wiki/history/{repoUrl}`, the full https URL, encoded). Page trees, pages, searches and the .zip export are read through `/api/factory/wiki/…` when the tab asks, and cached, since a run never changes once uploaded. **Generate** (`wiki-run.ts`) fetches `origin`, checks its default branch out as a detached worktree at the floor's `.droid-office/wiki-run/`, and runs `droid exec --auto high -m <model> "/wiki <rule>"` there with the office's Droid command and the office's Factory key as `FACTORY_API_KEY` in that one process's environment; `/wiki` uploads the wiki as its last step, to Factory only. The rule, in the prompt and in `--append-system-prompt`, says not to sync the repository's GitHub wiki tab, and `GIT_CONFIG_*` variables in the run's environment point git's URLs for that wiki repository at a transport that doesn't exist, so a sync fails before it reaches GitHub. A runtime `--settings` file beside the worktree (`.droid-office/wiki-run-settings.json`) sets every subagent complexity tier to the same model, so the owner's own subagent models don't apply to it. Once a run uploads, `.droid-office/wiki-run-models.json` remembers which model wrote it, since Factory's copy may not say. One run per floor. Its last lines (scrubbed of the key) go to browsers once a second, **Stop** ends its whole process tree, and the worktree is removed when it ends. A note in `.droid-office/wiki-run.json` says one is running: an office that stops mid-run ends it, and the next one cleans up after it and shows the run as lost.
- **Leaving.** A worker sent home finds its way to the exit door (or, on the floors above the bottom one, the balcony doors) along a route found on a half-meter grid of the floor (A*, then pulled tight), worked out in each browser when the office says the worker is gone.
- **The gong.** Each time the office fetches the PR list, a PR that was open last time and is merged now rings the gong. A merge from the PR window rings as soon as `gh pr merge` returns, and isn't rung again when the list catches up. Anything else shows up at the next fetch: about every 90 seconds while you're on the floor, and right after a queue task finishes. The gong is synthesized like the other sounds, so the volume and mute under **⚙️** → **Sound** apply to it.
- **Jukebox.** Each floor's `jukebox.json` says only what's on and when it started, so it keeps playing through a restart. Every window plays it for itself: a tune is decided note by note by its bar and step, so starting from the same moment is enough for every window to hear the same thing. To agree on that moment, each page pings the office a few times on arriving and keeps the quickest answer to line its clock up with the office's. A tune is drums, bass, an FM electric piano and a melody picked from the chord tones, with record crackle and tape wobble, in 32-bar rounds. It's scheduled a second ahead on a timer rather than each frame, which lets it carry on in a background tab. It goes through a panner at the jukebox and a filter that muffles it with distance. Most streams can't be routed through Web Audio without CORS, so a stream plays in a plain `<audio>` element, turned down by hand on the same distance curve. An audio file seeks to the shared position.
- **Floors.** `floors.json` in the office's `.droid-office/` lists the floors: each one's checkout and colors. The home folder (`~`) is always a floor, named **Home**: the office adds it after your projects at every start if it isn't there, and it has no 🗑 in the elevator and can't be taken off. It needn't be a git checkout, so it has no worktrees, and its own `.droid-office/` goes in `~`. Adding one takes a git checkout that already exists in the workspace folder (the office searches it up to four folders deep for `.git`, skipping hidden folders and `node_modules`) and records its path: nothing is cloned, copied or moved, and a path outside the workspace folder is refused. A floor whose origin is on GitLab gets GitLab boards: issues and merge requests come from GitLab's GraphQL API, and comments, merges, closes and assignments go through its REST API. Each floor runs its own workers, GitHub or GitLab boards, queue and changes watcher in its checkout. A floor you're not on, with nothing running, asks GitHub about its boards every 10 minutes instead of every 90 seconds. Each connection gets only the worker updates and terminal frames of the floor it's on.
- **The rooftop.** Nobody works up there, so the roof isn't a floor: it's a place (`@roof`, which no floor id can be) that you stand on, with none of a floor's workers, boards or queue. The DJ's set is endless and needs no state: its tracks, their keys, grooves and basslines, and where each one is follow from the office's clock, like the jukebox's tunes, so the set is always on the same bar, and the lights flash on the same kicks and snares even with the music off. Drum and bass at 172 BPM, synthesized in the browser: a two-step break, a reese bass through a filter that opens and closes on the eighths, a sub, pads, an arpeggio, risers into each drop and an air horn when one lands. Being drunk is just your own browser's: while you are, the frame is drawn into a texture and onto the screen through a shader that doubles, smears and ripples it. With reduced motion on, nothing sways or strobes.
- **The Mac app.** `src/desktop/main.ts` is Electron's main process. Unless it's already in an Applications folder, it first offers `app.moveToApplicationsFolder()`. It runs `bin/droid-office.js` as a child process on the app's own Electron, its Helper binary so the office gets no Dock icon (`src/desktop/runtime.ts`), with `ELECTRON_RUN_AS_NODE=1` (`src/desktop/office.ts`), waits for the office's page and loads it in a full-screen window. The office's environment is an interactive login shell's (`$SHELL -ilc` printing `env -0`, `src/desktop/shell-path.ts`) over the app's own, without that shell's `PWD`, `OLDPWD`, `SHLVL` and `_`, its `PATH` followed by the usual tool folders, and `LANG=en_US.UTF-8` when no locale is set. The office's PTY host inherits that variable; the commands written for workers (`office-queue`, `office-workers`, the Node fallback of the status hooks) set it themselves (`runAsNode` in `src/server/workers.ts`), and workers' own environment drops it, so an Electron app a worker starts runs normally. Nothing is packed in an asar archive, since those scripts and node-pty's `spawn-helper` run from disk. Quitting sends the office SIGTERM, a restart that keeps the workers; the window's "Leave the office?" guard (`src/client/leave.ts`) becomes a native prompt for Cmd+W and reload, and is skipped when the app replaces the page or quits. `src/desktop/updates.ts` is electron-updater pointed at this repository's public releases: it reads the Latest release's `latest-mac.yml` and installs the zip it lists through Squirrel.Mac, which accepts only an update signed by the same Developer ID.
- **Paired phones.** `src/server/devices.ts` keeps the phones in the office's `.droid-office/devices.json` (id, name, SHA-256 of the device token, `pairedAt`, `lastSeenAt`), written to a temporary file and renamed into place, mode 0600. Every HTTP request past the static files, and every WebSocket upgrade, goes through one gate (`access` in `src/server/server.ts`): a device token (`Authorization: Bearer`, else `?d=`) is checked against every stored hash with `timingSafeEqual`, and decides on its own, so a forgotten one is a 401 even on loopback; without one, it's loopback or the LAN token, as before. On `/ws`, a device token in the `Authorization` header skips the `Origin` check, because a browser can't put that header on a WebSocket; `?d=` doesn't skip it. For the same reason the two uploads that check the `Origin` (`/api/term/drop`, `/api/prompt/image`) accept the header too. `POST /api/mobile/pair` passes only the LAN gate (a device token doesn't pair another phone) and refuses a cross-origin `Origin`. The Phone window's own routes, `GET /api/mobile/pairing` and `DELETE /api/mobile/devices/<id>`, answer only loopback with a same-origin request (a matching `Origin`, or `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` on a GET) and no device token. Tailscale is read with `tailscale status --json` (2 seconds at most, cached 30 seconds, `src/server/tailscale.ts`); addresses in 100.64.0.0/10 count as Tailscale, never Wi-Fi, in the join URLs and the pairing code. A paired phone's open connection marks it seen at each 20-second heartbeat.
- **State.** The office's `.droid-office/` (in `~/droid-office`, or in the project you started it in) holds the floors (`floors.json`), the Jira connection (`jira.json`), the Factory connection (`factory.json`) and its credits ledger (`factory-credits.json`), the rewritten prompts and the Default worker (`prompts.json`), whether workers go home once their PR merges (`leave-on-merge.json`), the Subagents settings (`subagents.json`) and the paired phones (`devices.json`). Each floor's checkout has its own `.droid-office/` with the hook settings, the saved workers and their scrollback, the task queue, the pictures on the walls (`decor.json`), the jukebox (`jukebox.json`), the Jira epic (`jira-epic.json`), where the guests sit (`guests.json`), the cloud workers and the turn each is on (`cloud-workers.json`, mode 0600), what each lead hasn't heard from its subagents yet (`team.json`) and the meetings (`meetings.json`, and each meeting's notes in `meetings/`). It is added to `.git/info/exclude` automatically, so it never shows up in `git status`. An office from before this release may also have `accounts.json` and `chat.jsonl` there; both are left on disk and never read.

## Security notes

Anyone who reaches the office can drive Droid in that directory, and through it run commands as the user that runs the office. Treat the LAN token and the join URL like SSH access:

- The token is random per start, kept only in memory, and checked with one timing-safe compare per connection. Restarting the office mints a new one and cuts off the old join URL. Loopback connects freely, so anyone already on the machine is already in.
- Only enable `--trust-proxy` behind a proxy that appends `X-Forwarded-For` (Caddy and nginx both do). The office uses the rightmost hop. The loopback bypass reads the socket's own peer address, which headers can't spoof.
- Run the office as a dedicated, unprivileged user, in the project you mean to use.
- The WebSocket upgrade checks the `Origin` header and the LAN token. A paired phone's device token in the `Authorization` header stands in for both, since no web page can set that header on a WebSocket. The hook endpoint only listens on loopback and needs a random per-worker token.
- Device tokens are 256 random bits, shown to the phone once and kept only as SHA-256 hashes in `devices.json` (mode 0600), checked in constant time. Forget a phone in ⚙️ Settings → **Phone** and its token is refused at once. The pairing code and the list of phones are served only to the office's own page on its own machine.
- Service tunnels are relayed only to web servers a worker started, and only when the request passes the LAN gate. A server you started yourself outside the office is never listed or relayed.
- Workers don't inherit the LAN token or any parent agent-session variables.
- The automation relay (`/api/automation`) sits behind the same LAN gate. It takes JSON only and refuses any `Origin` but the office's own, so a web page open in your browser can't drive your office tab over loopback.
- The Jira API token is written to the office's `.droid-office/jira.json` with mode 0600 and is used only by the office's own read requests to Jira. It never goes to a browser, a log or a worker's environment, and Jira's error messages are scrubbed of it. A read-only token is enough, and is the safer choice: the office never writes to Jira, so it doesn't need a token that can.
- The Factory API key is written to the office's `.droid-office/factory.json` with mode 0600 and is used only by the office's own requests to Factory. It never goes to a browser, a log or a worker's environment, and Factory's error messages are scrubbed of it. A Factory feature's routes act on Factory as that key, so they sit behind the LAN gate, and anything that changes something must come from the office's own page or a paired phone, so a web page you have open can't drive Factory through your loopback.
- The picture fetcher (`/api/image`) sits behind the same LAN gate. It fetches any http(s) link it's given, from the office's machine. That gives nobody new reach: anyone at the office can already run `curl` from a shell worker. Pictures are served with a sandboxing `Content-Security-Policy`, so an SVG can't run script on the office's origin.

## Development

```bash
npm install          # also builds, and installs the pre-commit hook
npm run build        # vite (client) + tsc (server)
npm run lint         # Biome: lint and formatting check, fails on any warning
npm run format       # Biome: rewrite the files in the house format
npm run typecheck
npm test             # worker, queue, PTY and doc tests, and .env.example in sync
npm run test:coverage  # the same tests with a coverage report; needs Node 22.8+
node bin/droid-office.js /path/to/project
npm run desktop      # builds, then runs the Mac app from the checkout (it doesn't update itself)
npm run package:mac  # builds release/Droid Office.app, a dmg and a zip; signs with your Developer ID if the keychain has it
```

`npm run package:mac` notarizes the app too when `APPLE_ID`, `APPLE_APP_SPECIFIC_PASSWORD` and `APPLE_TEAM_ID` are set; `CSC_IDENTITY_AUTO_DISCOVERY=false npm run package:mac -- -c.mac.identity=null` builds it unsigned. electron-builder's settings are in [`electron-builder.yml`](electron-builder.yml), and the icon and entitlements in [`build/`](build).

**Lint and format.** [Biome](https://biomejs.dev) lints and formats every `.js`, `.mjs` and `.ts` file outside `dist/`, with the settings in [`biome.jsonc`](biome.jsonc): two-space indents, single quotes, semicolons, trailing commas and 240-column lines. `npm run format` fixes the formatting.

**Pre-commit hook.** `npm install` in a clone points git at [`.husky/`](.husky) (through the `prepare` script), and every commit then runs [lint-staged](https://github.com/lint-staged/lint-staged) and `npm run typecheck`. lint-staged runs `biome check --write` on the staged files, so formatting is fixed and added to the commit, and a lint finding stops it. The hook is a convenience; CI runs the same `npm run lint` and `npm run typecheck`. `git commit --no-verify` skips it, and `HUSKY=0` skips installing it. It isn't installed in CI (`CI=true`) or where dev dependencies aren't, like the packed release.

`npm run test:coverage` fails when line or function coverage of `src/` and `bin/` drops below the thresholds in its `package.json` script, and CI runs it in place of `npm test`. `npm test` still runs on Node 20.

`npm run dev` runs Vite with hot reload on :5173 and proxies to the server on :4600. Server edits restart the server, not the workers.

### Source hot reload with the normal local launch

For a local checkout started with **`npm run build && npm run start`**, open **⚙️ Settings → Building → Source hot reload** and enable it. The choice is saved in the office’s `.droid-office/hot-reload.json`. Enabling it builds once immediately; after that, saving client source, shared code, styles, HTML, public assets or the Vite/TypeScript build configuration triggers another build. It watches the checkout the office itself runs from, not whichever project floor is open. No separate dev server is needed.

The office checks for changes every second and waits briefly for saves to settle. It typechecks and builds the client in child processes into a private staging folder, then switches to the complete bundle and refreshes the browser. It does **not** replace individual live three.js modules: a full page refresh avoids stale scene objects, event listeners and incompatible module state after arbitrary game changes. The server, workers and PTYs keep running. Your saved browser settings, floor and position return, but open windows, unsent drafts and browser-only game state may need reopening.

A failed typecheck or build leaves the last successfully built office running and shows the error. Fixing the source retries automatically; **Build & reload** retries manually. A small recovery script is served independently of the game bundle and kept unchanged for the server’s lifetime, so even a game startup error can show **Retry build** and **Disable hot reload** and recover on the next successful build without a reload loop. Earlier hashed chunks stay available for tabs still running an older build. Temporary builds are removed on graceful shutdown; a force-killed server may leave its own `hot-reload-*` staging folder behind.

**Boundary:** this reloads browser game logic, visuals and UI, not running server modules or terminal lifecycles. Server changes, shared/server-side logic, protocol changes and dependency changes show a **server restart required** warning; run the normal build/start workflow to apply those consistently. A compilable client change can still contain a runtime bug or be incompatible with the old server—reload cannot guarantee arbitrary code is correct. Newly added dependencies must be installed normally. Source hot reload is unavailable in packaged installs without source or development dependencies. Vite’s :5173 dev page continues using Vite’s own reload instead.

### Automation API (`window.office`)

Every office tab has `window.office`, so an agent or a browser test can check a client change without steering by hand: no WASD, no lining up with a desk, no pressing E. Each command returns a promise. It resolves with a `state()` snapshot once the action has finished (you've arrived, the window is open, the ride is over). It rejects with an `Error` that says why: no such desk, stuck on the way, riding the elevator.

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `office.list()` | Everything `goTo` takes from here, each with `id`, `kind` (`desk`, `kiosk`, `board`, `place`, `floor`), `label` and, for a desk, its `worker` |
| `office.goTo(target, { walk?, timeout? })` | Puts you at a desk (behind the worker), a kiosk, a board or a place, facing it. `target` is a `list()` id (`desk-3`, `station-issues`, `issues`, `coffee`, `elevator`), a worker's id or name, a desk's label, a board agent's name, a floor's id or name, or `roof`. `{ walk: true }` walks there along the office's paths instead |
| `office.interact(key = 'E')` | Presses E, P, R, X, B, C or O at what you went to, or else at the nearest thing in reach, as if you faced it. E at a worker opens its terminal; at a board, it opens the board |
| `office.open(id)` | Runs a ☰ menu command: `issues`, `pulls`, `queue`, `services`, `meeting`, `search`, `elevator`, `roof`, `decor`, `settings`, `phone`, `help`, `upgrade`, `waiting` |
| `office.commands()` | Those commands, with whether each is offered here and why one is blocked |
| `office.closeAll()` | Closes every open window |
| `office.ride(floor)` | Takes the elevator to a floor (id or name) or `roof`, and resolves once the doors open there |
| `office.camera(preset)` | A fixed camera so screenshots compare between runs: `first`, looking straight ahead, level |
| `office.state()` | JSON for assertions: floor, position, facing and view, seat, what E would use (`using`), open windows (`modals`, by their labels), the open terminal, workers with desk and status, and floors |

With [agent-browser](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-browser) (`agent-browser eval --stdin`) or Playwright (`page.evaluate`):

```js
const o = window.office;
await o.goTo('Ada');                       // a worker's name, or 'desk-3'
await o.interact();                        // E: its terminal opens
await o.goTo('issues');
const s = await o.interact();              // E: the Issues board opens
s.modals;                                  // [{ label: 'Issues board', doing: '📋 at the issues board' }]
```

Wait for `window.office && office.state().floor` before the first command; a new browser profile shows the character picker first. If headless Chromium drops the page after a while (software WebGL), run the browser headed.

Without a browser driver, the server runs the same commands in the office tab opened last (not a phone), and answers with what the command resolved to or why it failed:

```bash
curl -s localhost:4600/api/automation -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"cmd": "goTo", "args": ["desk-3"]}'
# {"ok": true, "value": {…the state…}}  · 422 {"ok": false, "error": "…"} · 409 no tab open · 504 no answer in time
```

`args` is the command's arguments as a JSON array, and `timeout` (milliseconds, default 90 000) is how long to wait for the tab.

Rules for changing the code are in [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md). A directory with its own rules has an `AGENTS.md` beside the code; the root file lists them.

**Releases.** Every change to the app that lands on `main` is published as a GitHub release by [`.github/workflows/release.yml`](.github/workflows/release.yml), and `install.sh` installs the newest one. The workflow builds, lints and typechecks the office, runs the tests with the coverage thresholds, packs the release with an `npm-shrinkwrap.json` so every install gets the tested dependency versions, then installs the pack through `install.sh` and starts it before publishing. Alongside, a macOS job builds the Mac app at that version, signs it with the Developer ID from the repository's secrets (`MAC_CERTS`, `CERT_PASSWORD`), notarizes the app and its disk image with Apple (`APPLE_ID`, `APPLE_APP_SPECIFIC_PASSWORD`, `APPLE_TEAM_ID`), and starts the office on the app's own Electron. Only once both jobs pass does the release go up, marked Latest, with the tarball, the dmg, the zip and `latest-mac.yml`, which is what installed apps update from. Pull requests run the same steps, with an unsigned app, but publish nothing. A release is named after `package.json`'s major.minor and the number of commits on `main` (`v0.1.68`), so every release is a new version the app updates to; bump `package.json` to start a new minor version.
