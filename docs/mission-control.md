# Mission Control

The building's agents live in Mission Control, the glass room under the boss office. They belong to no floor and have no desk: they see and direct the whole building. There are two kinds, and they work the same way.

- **The Orchestrator** is the one the office launches and owns. You tell it what you want ("get CI green on every floor", "triage the open issues across my projects and put workers on the top three", "what is everyone doing?") and it plans, hires and directs workers on any floor, answers their questions, checks their work and reports back. It replaces the per-floor Team lead kiosk that was removed with the board agents (`f5023d2c`).
- **External bots** (⚙️ Settings → Phone & web → **Tailscale MCP**) run elsewhere, such as an OpenClaw agent, and reach the office over the owner's tailnet. They used to take a desk on one floor and move it with `office_move`. Now they sit at the Mission Control table like the Orchestrator, and that tool is gone.

## One model for both

- **Identity, not a seat.** A building agent is an identity in the office's own `.droid-office/remote-workers.json`, with its keys and inbox, and no floor or desk. Its `WorkerInfo` has `deskId: ''` and is never in a floor's worker list; clients get building agents only from Mission Control state. Records saved with a floor and desk load fine, and their desks are freed.
- **The same MCP surface.** Both sign in to the Office MCP's external surface: `office_floors`, `office_roster`, `office_look`, `office_send_home`, `office_worker_spawn` / `prompt` / `answer` / `interrupt` / `thread`, `office_inbox`, `office_whoami`, `team_models`, `team_hire` and the `team_*` / `worker_*` tools across floors. `office_move` is removed. Visits (`visit_message`, `visit_reply`) are left out: a building agent walks nowhere, and it reaches any worker with `office_worker_prompt`. Building agents are not on floor workers' `visit_roster`. `office_status` is listed for external bots only, because the office observes the Orchestrator's session itself.
- **Hiring names its floor.** There is no current floor. `team_hire` needs a structured task whose `repo` names an open floor (path, id or name), and `office_worker_spawn` needs `floor`. A subagent takes the free desk nearest Mission Control on its floor.
- **Full owner authority.** Either can hire, prompt, interrupt, answer menus and send home any worker on any floor. Worker-control actions name the agent in their toasts.
- **Their teams.** Subagents keep `lead: <agent id>` on their own floors and wear that agent's team markings. Dashes run from Mission Control to their desks. Removing a bot makes its subagents independent; turning the Orchestrator off leaves its team working, reporting to nobody until it is back on.

## The Orchestrator

- **One per office.** The office launches a single Droid session for it, in a PTY like any worker's, with the id `orchestrator` (`ORCHESTRATOR_ID` in `src/shared/mission-control.ts`). It starts on the first prompt, keeps its conversation across office restarts, and **New conversation** starts it over.
- **Its own loopback endpoint.** Its session reaches the external surface on a loopback-only endpoint that doesn't depend on Tailscale, so it works with `DROID_OFFICE_REMOTE_MCP=off`, in clones and in CI. Its key is its own, generated by the office and never shown in Settings → Tailscale MCP.
- **It never edits code.** It runs in the office's own `.droid-office/orchestrator/` folder, not a checkout. It reads code and plans, and every change goes to a worker it hires. Its brief says so, and the session is denied file-editing tools where Droid allows that.
- **Woken only by you and its own team.** Your prompt starts or steers it. When it is at rest and one of its subagents reports, asks, needs input or finishes, the office types a wake note into its session, as it does for a local lead. It doesn't watch the rest of the building by itself.
- **Needs you.** A finished turn you haven't read, or a question or permission prompt, counts toward desktop notifications, the tab title, the Mac menu bar and Android alerts, like a worker's (`orchestratorNeedsOwner`).
- **Settings.** ⚙️ Settings → **Orchestrator** (in the office and in the web, Mac and Android companions): on or off (on by default), its model and effort (office default unless chosen), and **New conversation**. Its brief is in the prompt editor under **🛰️ Orchestrator**. Saved in the office's own `.droid-office/orchestrator.json`.

## External bots

They keep their Tailscale endpoint, keys and polling (`office_inbox`, `team_wait`) as before. **Add bot** asks only for a name. Their status is what they last reported with `office_status`, `offline` until they report after an office restart. Opening one says it runs externally and shows its team, its last activity and when its key was last used.

## Protocol

`src/shared/mission-control.ts` holds the types. The server sends `missionControl` state in `welcome` (optional, so an older office simply has none) and a `{ t: 'missionControl', state }` message to every connection, on every floor, whenever a building agent or its team changes. Clients send `orchestrator.prompt`, `orchestrator.interrupt`, `orchestrator.reset` and `orchestrator.set`. The Orchestrator's terminal, thread and menus use the ordinary worker messages (`worker.attach` / `detach`, `term.input`, `term.resize`, `thread.read`, `worker.answer`) with `workerId: "orchestrator"`, accepted from any floor.

## Where it shows

- **3D office.** Mission Control is the same room on every floor. The Orchestrator stands at the head of the long table; each external bot has a chair at it. The wall panels show what the Orchestrator is doing and the building agents' teams across floors. Walk up to the Orchestrator to talk to it or open its terminal; walk up to a bot to see it. The ☰ menu, the palette and a key open the Orchestrator from anywhere on any floor.
- **Web and Mac companion.** A pinned Mission Control group at the top of the sidebar on every floor: the Orchestrator, then each bot, with their subagents (each tagged with its floor) beneath them. The Orchestrator's pane has Thread and TTY like a worker's, a composer that sends while it works and Stop, and an empty state that says what it is for.
- **Android.** A pinned Mission Control section on Home: the Orchestrator's card opens its Thread and terminal with a composer, and bots show their status and teams. Its questions are in Asks, with notifications when it finishes or needs you.
