# Mission Control

The building's agents live in Mission Control, the glass room under the boss office. They belong to no floor and have no desk: they see and direct the whole building. There are two kinds, and they work the same way.

- **The Orchestrator** is the one the office launches and owns. You tell it what you want ("get CI green on every floor", "triage the open issues across my projects and put workers on the top three", "what is everyone doing?") and it plans, hires and directs workers on any floor, answers their questions, checks their work and reports back.
- **External bots** (⚙️ Settings → Phone & web → **Tailscale MCP**) run elsewhere, such as an OpenClaw agent, and reach the office over the owner's tailnet. They sit at the Mission Control table like the Orchestrator.

## One model for both

- **Identity, not a seat.** External bot identities, keys and inboxes live in the office's own `.droid-office/remote-workers.json`; the Orchestrator has its own worker state. Both have no floor or desk. Their `WorkerInfo` has `deskId: ''` and is never in a floor's worker list; clients get building agents only from Mission Control state. Old bot records saved with a floor and desk load fine, and their desks are freed.
- **The same MCP surface.** Both sign in to the Office MCP's external surface: `office_floors`, `office_roster`, `office_look`, `office_send_home`, `office_worker_spawn` / `prompt` / `answer` / `interrupt` / `thread`, `office_inbox`, `office_whoami`, `whiteboard_read`, `team_models`, `team_hire` and the `team_*` / `worker_*` tools across floors. Visits (`visit_message`, `visit_reply`) are left out: a building agent walks nowhere, and it reaches any worker with `office_worker_prompt`. Building agents are not on floor workers' `visit_roster`. `office_status` is listed for external bots only, because the office observes the Orchestrator's session itself. External bots also exchange bounded, magic-byte-validated images (`PNG`, `JPEG`, `GIF`, `WebP`, up to 25 MB) over `POST /office/images` and `GET /office/images/<id>` on the same Tailscale Serve listener using `Authorization: Bearer <key>` (never tokens in URLs): uploaded 16-hex image ids attach to `team_message`, `team_hire`, `office_worker_prompt` and `office_worker_spawn`, while worker reports (`team_report`, `lead_notify`), `whiteboard_read` cards/PNG renders and `office_worker_thread` return `{ id, url, name, mimeType, bytes, width, height }` entries downloadable from `/office/images/<id>`.
- **Hiring names its floor.** There is no current floor. `team_hire` names an open floor with `floor` or a structured task's `repo`, each by id, name or checkout path; `office_worker_spawn` requires `floor`. A name two open floors share is refused with their ids, and so are a `floor` and a `repo` that name different floors. The other team tools take the same `floor` to tell apart workers with one name. A structured task's optional `baseBranch` selects its fresh worktree base. For read-only work, `worktree: false` uses the checkout and needs no invented base or branch. A subagent takes the free desk nearest Mission Control on its floor.
- **Owner authority over floor workers.** Either can hire, prompt, interrupt, answer menus and send home workers on any floor. Building agents cannot prompt, interrupt, answer or send home one another or themselves. `office_look` and `office_worker_thread` may read the Orchestrator; external bots have no local terminal or thread. `office_roster` lists building agents in a separate Mission Control section. Worker-control actions name the caller in their toasts.
- **Their teams.** Subagents keep `lead: <agent id>` on their own floors and wear that agent's team markings. Dashes run from Mission Control to their desks. Removing a bot makes its subagents independent. The Orchestrator's team stays its own through off, **New conversation** and an office restart: while it is off its subagents work on with nobody to report to, and between conversations their reports wait for its next one.

## The Orchestrator

- **One per office.** The office launches a single Droid session for it, in a PTY like any worker's, with the id `orchestrator` (`ORCHESTRATOR_ID` in `src/shared/mission-control.ts`). It starts on the first prompt, keeps its conversation across office restarts, and **New conversation** starts it over. A prompt for a session that has ended carries the conversation on, pictures included.
- **The local hook listener.** Its session reaches the building-agent surface through the existing loopback hook listener, independent of Tailscale, so it works with `DROID_OFFICE_REMOTE_MCP=off`, in clones and in CI. It authenticates with its own terminal hook token, not an external bot key. The token lives in its PTY environment and `.droid-office/orchestrator/.droid-office/workers.json` (mode `0600`); another worker's token cannot authenticate as `orchestrator`.
- **Scoped Office MCP approval.** Its process-local Droid settings contain a `PreToolUse` hook that returns `permissionDecision: "allow"` for the building agents' Office MCP tools and nothing else, matched by exact name in both spellings Droid uses (`droid-office___team_hire`, `mcp__droid-office__team_hire`). This lets owner-requested delegation run without waiting for an MCP approval in its deskless terminal. It does not approve shell commands, native tools, edits, other MCP servers or the Office MCP tools a building agent has no use of, and the built-in Task denial remains. The [official Droid hook contract](https://docs.factory.ai/reference/hooks-reference#pretooluse-control) defines this permission decision. Other permission prompts remain owner-answerable Asks; the office never treats an unanswered approval as an MCP request in flight.
- **It leaves code changes to workers and actively manages them.** It runs in the office's own `.droid-office/orchestrator/` folder, not a checkout. It plans, delegates repository changes through `team_hire`, and acts as its team's connective tissue: reading `team_models` once per batch, assigning disjoint single-writer file ownership, checking `worker_diff` / `worker_log` on wakes and check-ins, relaying contract changes across siblings, answering technical questions while escalating terminal permission prompts to the owner, and sequencing rebases before merging. Its file-editing tools are not denied, so it can write scripts in its folder and run shell commands (polling CI, starting servers, merging or opening pull requests) under Droid's ordinary permission prompts.
- **Woken only by you and its own team.** Your prompt starts or steers it. When it is at rest and one of its subagents reports, asks, needs input or finishes, the office types a wake note into its session, as it does for a local lead; news that arrives during a turn is handed over as that turn ends. Another lead's team never wakes it, and it doesn't watch the rest of the building by itself.
- **Needs you.** A finished turn you haven't read, or a question or permission prompt, counts toward desktop notifications, the tab title, the Mac menu bar and Android alerts, like a worker's (`orchestratorNeedsOwner`).
- **The floor you are on.** A prompt sent from a floor ends with a plain Droid Office notice line naming that floor and its checkout path, so "get all open PRs merged" means that repository. It rides with the first prompt of a conversation and again only when you prompt from a different floor; a prompt from Mission Control or the roof adds none. The brief calls it a default, never a restriction: a floor or repository the request names wins. Thread shows only what you typed.
- **Models it can hire.** Like floor leads and external bots, it follows ⚙️ Settings → Subagents & visits → **Models leads can pick** (`SubagentSettings.models`): `team_models` lists only the picked models with your notes, multipliers, efforts and subscription quota lines, and `team_hire` and `office_worker_spawn` (a cloud worker and the resolved default when `model` is omitted included) refuse any model outside the list, naming the allowed ids. With none picked it may hire any current model. Existing workers keep their saved models when resumed or messaged, and your own hires from a window are never limited. No building agent has a team size limit.
- **Settings.** ⚙️ Settings → **Orchestrator** (in the office and in the web, Mac and Android companions): on or off (on by default), its model and effort (office default unless chosen), and **New conversation**. A new model or effort takes over when its current turn ends: the office restarts its session on it and resumes the same conversation, and a session that has ended starts on it with its next prompt. Its brief is in the prompt editor under **🛰️ Orchestrator**, and a change to it is read the next time its session starts or resumes. Saved in the office's own `.droid-office/orchestrator.json`.
- **When it can't start.** Droid missing or signed out, the Office MCP off, or a model that can't run: `orchestrator.blocked` says why, and `orchestrator.blockedPane` names the Settings pane that fixes it (`connections` for Droid, `subagents` for the Office MCP; absent for its model, which is picked on its own pane). **Open Settings** in the office and **Settings** in the web companion open that pane. A session that is already running keeps its status and its Stop.

## External bots

They have a Tailscale endpoint, keys and inbox polling (`office_inbox`, `team_wait`). **Add bot** asks for a name, and optionally marks the bot as a **Grok Bot** with its HTTPS JSON webhook URL and `Authorization: Bearer` token (stored only in mode-`0600` `remote-workers.json`, never returned to clients). Each bot's main key and named keys share that bot's identity and webhook, so each Grok Bot connection wakes only its own bot, while non-Grok bots stay polling-only. For a Grok Bot lead, subagent progress and final `team_report` results, questions and blocked reports, mid-turn `lead_notify` pings, terminal `needs_input` asks, unexpected `stopped` / `done` / departure events, and inbox messages push immediately over HTTP `POST` as structured JSON alongside durable `office_inbox` delivery. Their status is what they last reported with `office_status`, `offline` until they report after an office restart. Opening one says it runs externally (marking Grok Bots with webhook push) and shows its team, its last activity and when its key was last used.

### Grok Bot webhook contract

Only Grok Bot connections receive pushes. Every accepted `team_report`, including `progress: true`, questions, and blocked/failed results, flushes immediately and returns a webhook receipt, unless the [noise gate](office-mcp.md#inter-agent-noise-gate) holds it for its batch or records it silently: those queue and ride along with the next push, and a routine `lead_notify` update is saved in `office_inbox` with `deferred: true` instead of pushing. Automatic terminal/status events and inbox messages share a 1.5-second coalescing window per lead. Non-test deliveries include `deliveryId` (`whk-…`) and `ackIds` at the envelope root so overlapping webhook runs can scope `office_inbox({ ids: payload.ackIds })` or `office_inbox({ deliveryId: payload.deliveryId })` (which still surfaces already-acknowledged items with `acked: true` and ignores out-of-scope acknowledgments). All items remain in the inbox until explicitly acknowledged; a failed push does not discard the report. A turn ending after a report does not produce a second `done` push.

The version-1 envelope and existing `report`, `question`, `blocker`, and `test` kinds remain. `progress` identifies incomplete reports (either `progress: true` or `result.status: "partial"`). Blocked and failed results use `blocker`. `pr` identifies pull-request transitions (opened, merged or closed) for subagents and `team_watch`-followed floor workers. Other existing kinds are `needs_input`, `stopped`, `done`, `left`, `sent_home`, `update`, and `message`. In a batch, `event` names the highest-priority item; process every entry in `items`.

`summary` leads with the actual outcome, progress or question (from `result.summary` or `text`), then the worker name. Content is whitespace-normalized and capped at 180 characters before the worker suffix, with an ellipsis when clipped. A batch leads with its highest-priority item's outcome and counts the other updates. Report items expose `taskComplete`; only a successful final report has `taskComplete: true`. Its worker snapshot is `status: "done", settled: true`, even while the reporting MCP call returns. This represents reported task completion, not independent verification, permission to merge, or a mutation of the terminal's runtime state. Other events retain their runtime status and settled values.

Structured reports carry content in `result.summary`, `result.artifacts`, `result.remaining`, and `result.conflicts`, without repeating the original prose in `text`. `reportId` retrieves that original report through `team_report_get`; the inbox also retains it. Questions keep `text` and `options`; an inferred result containing only the same question and its status is omitted. Distinct structured details remain. Legacy reports without a structured result retain `text`. The existing `id`, `ackId`, `reportId`, `questionId`, `prUrl`, `pushedSha`, and artifact aliases remain for compatibility and direct tool use. Redundant `reportPhase` is omitted (use `kind` and `taskComplete`), and optional `worker.state` is emitted only when it differs from `status`, such as `blocked_on_question`. `event` remains the batch discriminator, while each item's `kind` identifies its own event.

`actionHint` describes intent in plain words. Final reports with reported PR, commit, pushed SHA or changed-file artifacts request a recorded review before merging. Without change artifacts, hints ask the lead to read the outcome and follow up or dismiss the worker, with no review/merge prerequisite. A branch alone is not evidence of changes. Use the structured worker id, floor id, question id, and acknowledgment id rather than parsing tool syntax out of prose. Permission prompts remain owner-only.

Bearer credentials and the destination webhook URL are scrubbed from outgoing prose and nested result content. Fixed protocol values, timestamps, and opaque ids (including commit ids) stay intact even when a custom token coincides with a protocol word such as `test` or `team`. Raw and encoded credentials are matched longest-first in one pass, and error text is scrubbed before clipping. Credentials are only sent in the HTTP authorization header, never copied from connection settings into the payload.

Subagent connectivity test (no inbox entry or acknowledgment id):

```json
{"source":"droid-office","version":1,"event":"test","test":true,"summary":"[TEST] Droid Office webhook verification for grokbot.","sentAt":"2026-11-11T12:04:00.000Z","lead":{"id":"remote-example","name":"grokbot"},"items":[{"id":"test-example","kind":"test","at":"2026-11-11T12:04:00.000Z","floor":{"id":"alpha","name":"Droid Office","cwd":"/project"},"worker":{"id":"byte","name":"Byte","status":"working","settled":false},"text":"Connectivity verification","actionHint":"Connectivity verification ping from Droid Office; no action or acknowledgment required."}]}
```

**Send test** sends a test-only ping asking the receiving bot to reply to the owner with confirmation and all non-secret payload details, including event, source/version, lead, item ids and timestamps, plus any request id received (or its absence). The bot must omit authorization headers, keys, tokens and other secrets. Test pings require no `office_inbox` acknowledgment and do not start worker tasks; ordinary worker events retain their existing instructions.

## Protocol

`src/shared/mission-control.ts` holds the types. The server sends `missionControl` state in `welcome` (optional, so an older office simply has none) and a `{ t: 'missionControl', state }` message to every connection, on every floor, whenever a building agent or its team changes. The state's `orchestrator` holds its `settings`, its `team` (its subagents on every open floor, always present), its `agent` only while it has a session, and `blocked` when it cannot start. Clients send `orchestrator.prompt`, `orchestrator.interrupt`, `orchestrator.reset` and `orchestrator.set`. The Orchestrator's terminal, thread and menus use the ordinary worker messages (`worker.attach` / `detach`, `term.input`, `term.resize`, `thread.read`, `worker.answer`) with `workerId: "orchestrator"`, accepted from any floor.

## Where it shows

- **3D office.** Mission Control is the same room on every floor. The Orchestrator stands at the head of the long table; each external bot has a chair at it. The wall panels show what the Orchestrator is doing and the building agents' teams across floors. Walk up to the Orchestrator to talk to it or open its terminal; walk up to a bot to see it. The ☰ menu, the palette and **M** open the Orchestrator from anywhere on any floor.
- **Web and Mac companion.** A pinned Mission Control group at the top of the sidebar on every floor: the Orchestrator, then each bot, with their subagents (each tagged with its floor) beneath them. The Orchestrator's pane has Thread and TTY like a worker's, a composer that sends while it works and Stop, and an empty state that says what it is for.
- **Android.** A pinned Mission Control section on Office, and building-wide rows on the **Workers** tab: the Orchestrator's card opens its Thread and terminal with a composer, and bots show their status and teams. Its questions are pinned under **Needs you** on Workers, with notifications when it finishes or needs you.
