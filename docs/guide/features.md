# Features

## What's inside

- **Fork a worker.** Open a local Droid worker's terminal and click **Fork** between turns. Add an optional new direction, or leave it blank to wait for your next message. Its twin, named `Pixel ⑂` (then `Pixel ⑂ 2`), inherits the conversation in a new Droid session and gets a new branch and worktree at exactly the source's current commit, with its staged edits, unstaged edits and non-ignored untracked files. Workers across repositories fork each repository. The original stays untouched and can keep going. The twin counts toward the worker limit, sits at the nearest free desk (or an overflow bean bag), briefly splits away from the source, and stays connected by a dashed floor line while both are present. It is an independent peer, not a subagent. Ignored files such as `.env` follow the normal worktree setup, copied from the source's own worktree. Busy workers, permission prompts, shells, guests, cloud workers, and workers without a session or with a lost worktree cannot be forked.

- **A floor per project.** The first time the office starts in a terminal it walks you through picking your first project (see [Install & run](getting-started.md#install--run)). Otherwise you start inside the elevator, and it asks for one: pick one of the git checkouts the office finds in its workspace folder (a code folder in your home folder such as `~/Workspace` or `~/repos`, else the home folder itself; it looks up to four folders deep, so `<folder>/<owner>/<repo>` layouts work), or type a folder's full path, or press **📁 Browse** to walk the machine's folders and add the one you're in. **✕** or Esc closes that welcome panel so you can look around first; the elevator, or the name in the corner, brings it back, and a click on the dimmed office behind it doesn't. Git checkouts have to be inside the workspace folder. Any other folder, anywhere on the machine, can be a **plain floor**: its workers work in the folder itself, with no Issues, PR, Pipelines or CI boards, board agents, branches or worktrees. The Home floor works the same way. Pick another workspace folder with **Change folder** right there in the elevator, or in ⚙️ Settings → **Office & floors**. To add another project, or go to one, walk into the elevator on the north wall and press **E**. Or click the project name in the top-left corner for the list of floors: pick one and you're there in a blink, standing on the same spot in the office. Each floor has its own desks, workers, issues, PR and Pipelines boards and pictures, and its own wall and floor colors, so you always know where you are. Reload, or come back later, and you're on that same floor, standing where you were (or where you'd get up, if you were sitting) and facing the same way. A floor that's gone by then sends you up to the roof, and a note says which one. If something is in the way of your spot, you arrive in the elevator. The elevator panel shows how many workers are busy or waiting on someone on each floor, and you get a heads-up when a worker on another floor starts waiting. Take a project off the building with the **🗑** beside its floor in the elevator: its workers stop, and you ride the elevator to another floor. Nothing is deleted. The checkout stays on disk with its workers and pictures, and adding it again moves back into it. That goes for the project the office was started in (`droid-office <dir>`) too: it stays off after a restart, and the office keeps its own data in that folder as before.
- **Walk around.** Use WASD, Space to jump, and move the mouse to look around. Hold **↑** / **↓** to smoothly raise or lower your eye height, from the normal height (the default and minimum) up to **3×**. Arrow keys never walk or climb.
- **A quiet screen.** The office stays in view: the top bar holds just the floor you're on, **🤖 Workers** and the **☰** menu (or **Tab**). The menu opens every window (issues, PRs, pipelines, the Whiteboard, search, settings) and lists what can show on screen: the workers and the floor details. Turn each one on or off there, or hide a panel with its **✕**. Pin anything you use a lot to the top bar. The choices are kept in your browser. What needs you shows up on the top bar by itself: the workers waiting on someone (click to go to them, like **N**), and **⬆️ Update** when there's a new version.
- **Boss office.** Stairs along the back wall climb to a glass-walled office on the loft in the corner. From up there you can look down over every desk and watch your workers go.
- **Ladder.** The floors are stacked like a real building, with a ceiling over each. A ladder up the west wall goes through trapdoors in the ceiling and the floor: press **E** to grab it, **W** / **S** to climb, and **E** (or **Space**) to get off. Climb past the ceiling and you come up through the floor of the floor above.
- **Upstairs, over the garage.** The office is the second floor. Its windows are real glass, so you can look out over the street, the trees and the neighbours. Walk out the EXIT door in the west wall and down the stairs to the street. Underneath is an open garage full of Lambos and Ferraris, and you can jump up onto them.
- **A tower that grows with your projects.** Every floor is a storey of the same building, so from outside it's as tall as you have projects: add enough of them and it's a skyscraper, a balcony off every floor, windows lit here and there at night and a cornice round the top. Up on a higher floor the street is that many storeys further down. Only the bottom floor has the EXIT door and the stairs down; the floors above it have wall there, so take the elevator down to leave.
- **Night city, and weather.** It is always night: the building is barely lit, a cyberpunk basement where most of the light comes from the laptops' screens, with the wall boards dimly lit but easy to read. Outside, the street lamps, the balcony's string lights and the windows across the street glow. The weather comes and goes by itself (clear, clouds, rain, thunderstorms, fog, and snow in winter), with wet streets and snow settling outside. The haze and fog stay outdoors: the office and the garage are clear inside, though you see the weather through their windows, and up on the roof it's all around you. Start the office with `--city Berlin` and the sky follows that city's live forecast instead. Every window shows the same sky, and **⚙️** → **Office & floors** shows what it's doing.
- **The balcony.** An open doorway in the south wall leads out onto a balcony over the garage, with string lights, a bench and a bistro table.
- **Take a seat.** Press **E** at the lounge couch, a beanbag, the balcony bench or a stool at its bistro table, or the couch or the boss's chair up in the boss office to sit down. Walk off, jump or press **E** again to get up. In the boss's chair, **E** opens the shell on the monitor instead; walk off or jump to get up.
- **A shell on the boss's monitor.** The monitor on the boss's desk is a login shell in this floor's checkout, and it stays on: dev servers, git, tests. Nobody sits at the desk, and it isn't a worker. Sit in the boss's chair and press **E** to open it, the same terminal as a shell worker. Close it and the shell keeps running, on the monitor and for anyone else who opens it. `exit` starts another one.
- **Pick your character.** The first time you open the office, a character select screen lets you choose your skin tone, hair style, hair color and shirt, with a spinning preview. **✕** or Esc goes in with the look and name picked so far. Change it any time from **⚙️** → **You**. Settings is a wide window with categories down the side, grouped as Personal (You, Sound, Display, Notifications), Workers (Workers, Subagents & visits) and Office (Connections, Office & floors, Phone & web), each headed with who it applies to — just you, everyone, or the whole office — and on a phone that list is a row across the top. It reopens on the last category, and arrow keys (and Home and End) move between them. The search at the top of the list finds a setting by name or by what it does (try “jira epic”, “hide models” or “hot reload”) and jumps straight to its row.
- **Hire workers.** Walk up to an empty desk and press **E** to hire a Droid worker, or press **P** to write a task first. A little worker sits down, a laptop opens, and the agent's live screen appears on it.
- **Open a screen from across the room.** Shoot it with the droid wand or .44 Magnum to open its UI, with no distance limit: worker laptops, the boss's monitor, Issues, PRs, Pipelines, CI automations, the Whiteboard, the compute wall, pictures, the bookshelf, the elevator panel and the jukebox. A laptop opens its worker's UI without hurting it; the boss's monitor opens its shell without seating you. The Pipelines board opens the run you hit, and the Whiteboard opens the card it is showing. Shooting the Issues board opens the board, not a card to carry. Furniture and other solid objects still block shots. Physical controls such as seats, the gong and the ladder still need **E** nearby.
- **Bean bags for the overflow.** There are 16 desks. Once they're all taken, a bean bag comes out on the floor with a low lap desk in front of it. Hire a worker there the same way as at a desk, and the next bean bag comes out, up to 12 around the room. A bean bag stays out while a worker sits on it, and the empty one goes away again as soon as a desk frees up.
- **Shells.** Press **B** at an empty desk to open a plain login shell for dev servers, git or tests. It works like any worker's terminal: open it from another window and it streams there too.
- **Guests: agents you run yourself.** Start `droid` (or `claude`, `codex`, `opencode`, `grok`, `muse`) in a terminal of your own, in one of the floors' checkouts, and within a few seconds a guest sits down at a free desk on that floor with a name tag, its provider, and for droid its model, session title and status. Nobody hires it. It counts on the top bar, under **N** and in the elevator's per-floor counts like any worker, and goes home when its process exits. The office only watches a guest: it has no terminal in the office, so **E** at its desk shows what the office knows (the pid, the terminal it's on, its folder and where its status comes from) and takes no typing. It's never sent home, prompted, handed a task, shot, pruned or counted against the worker limit; type to it and quit it in its own terminal. To work with a droid guest in the office instead, **bring it in**: **R** at its desk (or **🚪 Bring it in** in its window) quits its droid outside, whose terminal goes back to the shell, and the same session carries on at the same desk, with the same name, as one of the office's own workers, its terminal opening right away. That works for a droid guest in the floor's checkout itself whose session the office knows, between turns; otherwise its window says why not. A droid guest's status comes from its session transcript: *working* mid-turn and *done* once its turn ended. A permission prompt looks like working from there, and a guest whose transcript the office can't tie to it (another CLI, or two droids in one folder) just shows *running*. A droid you start by hand in a worker's own worktree is that worker's: its desk hint says so instead of seating a second guest.
- **Isolated branches.** When you hire with a task, you can tick *own git worktree*. The worker then gets its own `office/<name>` branch under `.droid-office/worktrees/`, so parallel workers never share a checkout. The branch starts from the latest floor branch on the forge (the repository default unless you pick another in the top-left floor menu), whatever branch the floor's checkout is on, so a task includes pull requests merged since you last pulled. The floor's own checkout is never moved for a worktree. If it checks out or renames a branch of its own, the office follows that branch for the pull request and for sending it home. A branch that was already there, like main, is never deleted, and commits still only on the office branch are kept. When you send that worker home you choose whether to keep the worktree and branch, delete the worktree, or delete both, and the office warns you first about uncommitted changes or commits no remote has. `droid-office prune` clears out whatever was kept, once it is safe. Under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Workers whose pull request merged*, have them go home by themselves once their pull request (or GitLab merge request) merges: as soon as one isn't working or waiting on you and none of your windows has its terminal open, it leaves and its worktree and branch are deleted. Uncommitted changes, or commits that aren't on the remote, keep the worktree, as when you send it home. It's off until you turn it on, and turning it on sends home the ones already merged.
- **Choose the floor branch.** Click the project name at the top left, choose a **Floor branch**, and click **Switch branch**. Pick a local or origin branch to make it the base for new workers and their pull requests, or **Repository default** to go back to the repository's default branch. Each floor remembers its choice across restarts. Switching preserves uncommitted work and refuses to move a checkout another worker or guest is using. Existing worktrees stay on their task branches, and subagents still start where their lead says. Workers' checkout-return instructions and the automatic return after a merged PR use the selected floor branch, not a hard-coded main.
- **Fresh workers start from the floor branch.** A worker you hire starts from the latest of the selected floor branch, or the repository default (`origin/HEAD`, else `main` or `master`). In its own worktree, that's where the worktree is cut. Without one, it works in the floor's checkout, so the office first brings that checkout to the latest floor branch: it switches over from another branch and fast-forwards to origin's copy. It never stashes, resets or throws anything away to do it. The checkout stays as it is, and a toast says why, when another worker, shell or guest is working in it, when it has uncommitted changes on another branch, when a merge or rebase is under way, when `main` has commits origin doesn't have, or when git refuses the switch (an untracked file in the way). Uncommitted changes on `main` itself come along when git can carry them. Board agents at the kiosks don't count as working in it, and they start as the checkout is. Subagents are left out of that: a subagent's worktree starts where its lead is (the lead's own `HEAD`, so it has what the lead committed, moved up to origin's copy of that branch when the lead is strictly behind it, so a lead on an old `main` doesn't hand it that) or from the branch, tag or commit the lead names with `office-workers hire --from`, and the checkout is never moved for one. Once the branch the checkout is on has merged, the office moves the checkout back to the latest floor branch by itself, even if the worker that merged it never switched back (see *Fresh workers and the checkout*). Every worker is also told, in its system prompt, to leave the checkout on the latest floor branch after it merges, and the prompts the boards send for a merge say so as a last step. ⚙️ **Settings** → **Workers** → *Where fresh workers start* switches it to *Checkout's branch*: worktrees are then cut from the branch the checkout is on and the checkout is never moved, unless the floor has an explicit branch override. It's on until you turn it off, the same on every floor.
- **Worktrees that are ready to work in.** A new worktree is set up before its worker starts: the office copies the untracked files it needs from the floor's checkout (its `.env` files, by default) and runs the project's setup command in the worker's terminal, so you can watch it and the agent starts once it's done. The command is the one in a `.droid-office.json` committed at the repository's root (`{ "worktree": { "setup": "npm ci", "copy": ["/.env"] } }`); without one, the office goes by the lockfiles it finds (`npm ci` for `package-lock.json`, `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile`, `yarn install`, `bun install`, `uv sync`, `poetry install`, `bundle install` and others). `copy` and a `.worktreeinclude` file take gitignore patterns of files to copy. Under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Worktree setup* you see what a new worktree on the floor you're on gets and where that came from, can set the floor's own command and files in place of the repository's, and **Have a worker set it up…** opens the hire window at the nearest free desk with a task to write the repository's `.droid-office.json`, test it from scratch and open a pull request with it; pick its model, edit the task, and hire. When the setup fails, a toast says so, the agent starts anyway, and its first message tells it so.
- **One task across several projects.** When you hire a worker in its own worktree, **🗂️ Also work in** lists the building's other floors: pick the projects the task spans (in the hire dialog, or in **🤖 Hand to a worker** / **✍️ Ask a worker** from the boards). The worker starts in a workspace folder with a worktree of each project, all on the same `office/<name>` branch, and a short brief (`AGENTS.md`, the **🗂️ Across repositories** prompt in Settings) saying which folder is which. **O** opens a pull request in every project it committed to, and each description lists the others. The issue the task came from is closed by the home floor's pull request and mentioned from the others. Press **O** again for each project's pull request. The Changes window has a tab per project. Sending the worker home checks and deletes every worktree with the same branch rules as a worker in one project, and *Workers whose pull request merged* waits until every project's pull request has merged.
- **One-click PRs.** When a worktree worker is done, press **O** at its desk. The office pushes the branch the worktree is on (including one the worker created itself) and opens a pull request with a title and body drafted from the task (work handed over from the issues board gets a `Closes #n`). The PR shows up on the PR board right away, tagged with the worker's desk, and **Go to desk** takes you there. Press **O** again to see the PR.
- **Live terminals.** Press **E** at an occupied desk to open the real terminal (a PTY, over WebSockets). Open the same one from a second tab or another browser and it streams in each; closing one window leaves the others attached. Drop a file onto the open terminal, or paste a screenshot, and the office keeps it under `.droid-office/drops/` and types the path in, the way a native terminal does with a dragged file. The PTY takes the size of whichever window typed into it last.
- **Prompt history and chips.** A prompt that goes to an agent (hiring, prompting a worker, a board agent or the Team lead, **🤖 Hand to a worker** / **✍️ Ask a worker**, a cloud worker) has a row of chips above it: **Review**, **Tests**, **Lint**, **Explain** and **Commit**. A chip fills the box, and you can edit the text before it sends. Change the names and the wording, add your own (up to 12) or clear the row under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Prompt chips*. The last 50 prompts you sent are kept in this browser, newest first. **↑** recalls the previous one while the cursor is on the first line, and once one is recalled **↑** and **↓** keep walking until you move the cursor into the text. **↓** from the last line returns to what you were typing. **History** beside the box opens the list to pick from. Esc closes the History list first and leaves the prompt and your draft in place; press Esc again to close the window. A shell command, a commit message and a pull-request description aren't kept.
- **Pictures in prompts.** Paste a screenshot (or drop picture files onto the window) in any prompt that goes to an agent: hiring a worker, prompting one, a board agent or the Team lead, and **🤖 Hand to a worker** / **✍️ Ask a worker**. Each picture shows as a small numbered thumbnail under the box, and a ✕ appears when you hover it; removing one renumbers the rest. PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, up to 10 per prompt and 25 MB each. A picture alone is enough to send. The agent gets your text, then a blank line and one line per picture, like `Image 1: /path/to/shot.png`, so you can say "image 2" and it knows which; it opens the files itself. The office keeps each picture under `.droid-office/drops/staged/` from the moment you paste it and deletes it when you take it out or close the window without sending. Once the prompt is sent, each worker has its own copy under `.droid-office/drops/<worker id>/`, which goes when the worker does. The *Run in shell*, commit and pull request boxes take no pictures, since they aren't prompts to an agent.
- **Changes at the desk.** Press **C** at an occupied desk (or click **🌿 Changes** in its terminal) to see what the worker changed: the changed files and their diff against the newer of the branch the office was opened on and that branch on the remote, refreshed every couple of seconds while it works. A worker across several projects has a tab per repository, each against that floor's own branch. From there you can commit, discard, or push the branch and open a pull request; the commit message and the pull request's title and description start from the worker's task card. **Commit & open PR** asks the worker itself to finish the job: it types the **Commit & open PR** prompt (⚙️ Settings → Workers → Prompts) to the worker, for the repository tab you are on, with the title and summary from its task card, and the window switches to its terminal so you can watch it commit, push and open the pull request (or push to the one already open). A worker with no task card yet gets a box to say what the work is first. **T** switches the window between the diff and the worker's terminal, read-only, without leaving it; **Terminal** leaves Changes for the terminal window, to type to the worker.
- **Live status.** Droid's hooks drive each worker's status: *working*, *needs input* or *done*. When a worker needs a human or has finished, it jumps up and down and you hear a short tone: rising for needs input, two notes up for done. Tests or a build failing twice, or an error, plays two low notes instead. Mute just those under **⚙️** → **Sound** → *Alert sounds*; muting the office mutes them too. Its antenna bulb shows the status from across the room.
- **Workers act out what they're doing.** A working worker acts out its latest tool call, so you can tell what it's doing from across the room without reading its card. Reading files, it holds up a stack of papers and flips through them. Editing, it hunches over the keys and types flat out. Running tests or a build, it leans back with its hands behind its head. Searching or fetching the web, a globe spins beside its laptop. When its tests or build fail twice in a row, it puts its head in its hands. Waiting on you, it jumps for a couple of seconds, then crosses its arms and taps its foot. When it finishes, it does a little spin and a puff of confetti goes up. Droid's hooks tell a shell command that runs tests from one that only reads, and they report failures.
- **Next worker that needs you.** Press **N** to go straight to the worker that has waited longest on someone (it needs input, or it's done and nobody has looked), standing behind it and looking at its laptop. Press **N** again for the next one, oldest first, and after the last you start over. A pin at the edge of the screen points the way to each waiting worker you can't see, red for *needs input* and green for *done*, and the top bar counts them (`🙋 2 waiting · ✅ 1 done`; click it to do the same as **N**). With nobody waiting on your floor, **N** tells you which floor has someone.
- **Notifications.** You don't have to be in the office tab. While the tab is in the background, its title counts the workers waiting on someone, like `(2) my-project · Droid Office`, and the count clears when you come back. Allow desktop notifications (the office asks when you first hire a worker, or turn them on under **⚙️** → **Notifications**) and a worker that needs input or finishes while you're in another tab or app pops one up. Click it to jump straight to that worker's terminal.
- **Task cards.** A card over each worker's head names what it's on ("Fix Login Redirect") and says in one line what it's doing right now. A small model (Haiku, through `droid exec` — the Droid the office already needs) writes it from the worker's prompts and recent tool calls. Without it, the card falls back to the prompt itself.
- **Survives restarts.** Workers are saved to disk with their model choice and session ID, and their terminals run in a small process of their own. When the server restarts (a dev-server reload, a `kill` of its process), the agents keep working through it, and the new server picks every terminal back up, scrollback and all. Open terminals reconnect by themselves. Ctrl+C stops the workers; the next start wakes them back up in their sessions, and **R** resumes the Droid session of one that exited. When a restart does take a worker's terminal with it (the machine rebooted, the terminal process died or was replaced by a newer one), a worker that was in the middle of something is resumed with *continue*, so it carries on by itself. Each worker's last 3,000 lines of terminal are kept too, so after a restart (or a crash) you can still scroll back through what the workers printed.
- **Search.** Press **/** (or **🔎 Search** in the **☰** menu) to search every worker's terminal at once, including what they showed before the office restarted. Click a terminal line to open that terminal scrolled to it, with the line lit up.
- **Command palette.** Press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a Mac) and type a few letters to find a worker, an issue, a pull request, a board or an action. **Enter** opens it. **Shift+Enter** walks you there first, then opens it. Up on the roof, or when it isn't a place you can stand, **Shift+Enter** opens it the same way **Enter** does.
- **Compute wall.** A big screen on the west wall, between the exit door and the kitchen, shows how busy the office's machine is on its left end: CPU and memory right now, the last five minutes of each, and how many workers the office runs. The rest of it is your Factory Droid Computers once the office is connected to Factory (below): a tile per computer with its status (active, asleep, provisioning with the step it's on, waking, error), whether Factory runs it or it's your own machine (BYOM), and for a managed one its CPU, memory and disk now and over the last six hours. When the office's own machine is one of them (a BYOM computer named after it), the machine's end wears a *Droid Computer* badge instead of it getting a tile of its own, and the window marks it *This machine*. Up to six computers get big tiles, then they go to compact rows. Not connected, it says how to connect; if Factory is down or turns the key away, it keeps the last computers it read with their age. The cubes in the rack under it are your computers too, lit when they're active.
- **Droid Computers window.** Walk up to the compute wall and press **E** (or **☰** → **Computers**, or the command palette) for every Droid Computer: its status, provider, load and repositories. Click one for its details, its provisioning steps with how long each took, and CPU, memory and disk charts over 6 hours, 24 hours or 4 days. A managed computer has **Wake** (resume it if it's asleep), **Reboot**, **Keep awake**, **Refresh credentials** (its git credentials and the organization's secrets), **Retry install deps**, rename and remote user, and **Delete**, which takes its name typed out. **+ New computer** makes one, or several at once with a name prefix, picking the repositories it clones from those Factory's GitHub app sees (this floor's repository is picked to start with). **Overview**, at the top of the list, is the fleet at a glance (how many are active, asleep, starting or in error, and a card per computer with its load) and the **Computer secrets**, the environment variables every computer in the organization gets: their names, a way to set one and remove one. Values are write-only and never shown. A computer started from an Environment wears its name, and **+ New computer** can start one from an Environment.
- **Environments.** The Computers window's second tab (or **☰** → **Environments**) is your Factory Environments: what Factory builds once (a base image, your repositories with an install script each, and a machine setup script) so a computer started from one is ready in seconds. Each shows its build (queued, building, ready, updating, failed), its stages and log while it builds, who can use it (the organization or just you), the computers started from it and its scripts, with **+ Computer from it**, **Rebuild**, **Stop build**, **Make default**, **Edit** and **Delete** (its name typed out). **+ New Environment** makes one, picking its repositories from those Factory's GitHub app sees (this floor's to start with). The overview lists every computer started from an Environment, and flags the ones whose Environment was deleted, which keep running until you delete them.
- **Worker limit.** Start with `--max-workers <n>`, or set a limit under **⚙️** → **Workers** → *Worker limit*, and the office runs at most that many workers at once across every floor (shells and board agents count too). Hiring past it is refused with a message saying why. In the office, the limit can be lowered but never raised past `--max-workers`. When memory is 90% used, or the CPU has been 90% busy for half a minute, the compute wall says the machine is under pressure, the hint at an empty desk says so, and the hire dialog warns you before you hire.
- **Issues board.** A tack board shows GitHub issues in *Open*, *In progress* and *Closed*. Click a column's header to filter it by label: pick one or more and the column shows only the cards with any of them. Your filters stay put for each floor, in your browser. Or type in the box under a column's header to narrow it to the cards whose titles have every word you type (any case, any order). It works along with the label filter, the count shows how many of the column's cards match, **✕** clears the box, and the boxes start empty each time you open the board. Click an issue to read it and its comments, or add a comment of your own (it posts as the account `gh` is signed in as on the server; the window names it), then choose **Hand to a worker** to seat a worker with a ready-made prompt. Or take its card straight off the board, without opening it: point the crosshair at the note and press **E** or click (**O** opens it instead). **✋ Pick it up** in an issue's window does the same. You carry the card across the office in your hands, and it stays missing from the board until you put it back: press **E** at an empty desk to hire a worker for it, or at a worker's desk to hand it to that worker. A worker that gets an issue, through **Hand to a worker** (a new worker or one already at a desk) or its card, gets the issue assigned on GitHub, so it moves to *In progress*. **Q** puts the card back. **Close issue…** closes it as completed or not planned, with an optional comment. **Edit** next to an issue's labels (**Add labels** when it has none), or the **Labels** button that shows when you hover its card on the board, picks its labels from the repo's own: tick them on and off, filter by typing (**Enter** ticks the first match), then **Save labels**. The change goes out through the server's `gh` or `glab` account. An *in progress* (or *doing*, *wip*) label moves the issue to *In progress*.
- **Jira board.** Connect the office to Jira Cloud once, under **⚙️** → **Connections**, with a site, an email and an [API token](https://id.atlassian.com/manage-profile/security/api-tokens). A read-only token (scope `read:jira-work`) is enough, since the office only reads Jira. The main commands:

    | Command | What it does |
    | --- | --- |
    | `read [<card>…] [--json] [--png <dir>]` | The whole board, or the cards named: HTML, link, state, threads, stickies, pictures, with `CHANGED` on what someone else changed since this worker last read that card. `--png` writes an image of each card with its marks |
    | `push --title "…" [--file f \| --dir d \| --link f \| --image f \| --canvas]` | A new card from stdin, a file with its local assets, a folder package, a linked file the card follows, an image or a blank canvas |
    | `update <card> …`, `link`, `unlink`, `rm` | Replaces a card's content or title in place (marks reattach), links or unlinks it, or deletes it |
    | `state <card> [get \| set \| merge \| del]` | Reads or writes the card's state: form answers, button presses and the page's own data |
    | `comment`, `sticky`, `picture <card> …` | Adds, replies to, resolves, edits, moves, reattaches and removes marks, anchored to a `data-wb-id`, a CSS selector with a quote of its text, or a point |
    | `show [<card> \| --grid]` | Sets what the wall shows |
    | `apply` | Any list of operations as JSON on stdin, applied all at once or not at all |

  - **The artifact JavaScript API.** The office puts `window.whiteboard` into every card's page: `whiteboard.state.get(path?)`, `.set(value)`, `.merge(obj)` and `.del(path)`, `whiteboard.on('state', fn)` whenever it changes (at once for the page's own writes, so a page that draws from its state updates as you click), `whiteboard.action(name, data?)` to record the last press of a named action (a `<button data-wb-action="approve">` does it with no script, and the window confirms the press with a toast), `whiteboard.card` (its id, title and author) and `whiteboard.rendering`, which is `true` when the renderer loaded the page, so it can skip side effects. Form fields with a `name` or `id` are saved into `state.fields` and come back on load with no script at all.

- **Wand spells.** With it drawn, **1–5** (and the **G** wheel) are its spells. **1** Lumos lights the tip and the room around you (again for Nox, or put it away). **2** swish and flick briefly levitates the worker directly under the crosshair, then returns it to its seat at full size, without interrupting its task. **3** Sparkler starts the floor's desk-dancing and confetti, like the celebration when a PR merges, but does not merge anything or change workers' tasks. **4** Ship it requires aiming directly at one worker within **2.5 meters**: the confetti pops on that worker and immediately sends it an intent to commit and push to main, or merge its PR. - **The .44 Magnum (opt-in).** With **.44 Magnum** picked in **⚙️ Settings → You**, press **7** to draw it and click a worker to open its terminal, just like the wand. - **Charged shot.** With the wand or Magnum drawn, **hold left-click for 0.75 seconds, then release** to dismiss a worker: the worker goes straight to heaven, its session stops, and its owned worktree and branch are deleted, **including uncommitted changes and unpublished commits**. A tap or early release fires normally; a full charge waits for you to release. A ring fills around the crosshair, the weapon pulls back, and a rising tone and ready cue tell you when it is charged. On a MacBook's Force Touch trackpad (in the Mac app, or a browser on the office's own Mac) you feel it too: a hard tick as the ring crosses each eighth, a firm triple click when it fills, and a strong buzz under your finger when the charged shot is released. The patterns follow your Mac's trackpad haptic setting; anywhere else the charge is sound and ring only. The Magnum releases a white-hot blast with pressure rings, heavier recoil, smoke and a deep report; the wand thrusts a pale-gold double-spiral bolt into a crowned, sparkling sigil with a resonant magical snap. Holstering, changing weapons or spells, opening a window, Escape, or losing focus cancels a held charge and its sound without firing. A screen hit still opens its UI, and furniture still blocks the shot; guests and cloud workers remain untouched. Right-click does not fire.
