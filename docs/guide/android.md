# Droid Office for Android

**Droid Office for Android** is a companion app for your phone: it lists the workers on each floor, hires new ones at any free desk, shows their live terminals, lets you prompt them or type into them, and shows the floor's Whiteboard. It talks to the same office over the same WebSocket as the browser. How to build and install it is in [`android/README.md`](../../android/README.md).

**App updates.** The app quietly checks the latest public GitHub release when you open it, and tells you when a newer Android app is available. Under **Offices → About**, or on the welcome screen before pairing, **Update app → Update now** downloads the APK with progress and opens Android's installer. Allow installs from Droid Office the first time, confirm the update and reopen the app. Your paired offices stay put. You can cancel the download or keep using the app while it runs. **Download newest version** opens the latest release in a browser if in-app updating doesn't work.

**Tablets and foldables.** On a tablet or an open foldable, the app's **Office** tab is the floor as a live 3D map, the same Overview as the web companion's: workers at their desks with their terminals on their laptops, a roster beside it, and a dock to recenter, zoom and hire. Tapping a worker flies in to its desk and opens its terminal; tapping a free desk twice hires there (`worker.spawn` with that `deskId`). Held upright the roster runs along the bottom; a foldable half open keeps the map on one side of the fold, above it (tabletop) or on one page (book). A phone, upright or sideways, a foldable's cover screen and a WebView without WebGL 2 keep the worker list. The map is a page bundled in the app, built from the office's map code by `node tools/android-overview.mjs`; the app feeds it the floor, its workers and their screens, and fetches the models and fonts it asks for from the office's open `/props/` and `/fonts/` routes itself.

**Worker views.** Opening a worker defaults to **Raw TTY**, with its terminal and quick keys. A connected local Droid worker waiting on a question with a readable answer menu opens in **Thread** instead, so you can answer from its card. A waiting prompt the phone can't answer stays in Raw TTY, as does a worker opened with a draft answer to type. You can switch views, and your choice stays put while you are there.

**Direct typing.** In Raw TTY, tap **Direct** beside **Phone** to type straight into the terminal instead of the composer's input bar. Each character, Backspace and Enter reaches the worker immediately, with no Send step. Hardware keyboards work too. Tap the terminal to reopen the keyboard, or turn Direct off to return to your saved composer draft and pictures.

**Quick keys.** Raw TTY's quick keys send Ctrl+C, Ctrl+D, Ctrl+X, Option+Up and Down, the arrows, Enter, Tab, Shift+Tab, Esc, Backspace and `/`. The sliders button at the end of the row opens **Arrange the keys**, where you drag keys into the order you want or move them with the arrows; the phone keeps that order through app updates.

**The Whiteboard.** The app's **Board** tab is the current floor's [Whiteboard](../whiteboard.md): the grid of cards newest first, with the office's thumbnails and a mark on each card someone else changed since you looked. A card opens live, as it does in the office's window: its videos play and its forms and buttons work, with **Use** and **Mark up** as in the window, where a tap pins a comment and a long-press places a sticky note. Stickies and pictures move, resize and reattach, a picture comes from the phone's photos, and **Comments** has the card's threads (reply, resolve, reopen, show, move) and sticky notes. A phone whose WebView is too old shows the card as the renderer captured it, where a long-press pins a comment. Opening a card moves your read point, as opening it in the window does; the wall button shows that card on the wall (or the grid again), and **Ping a worker** sends the same `whiteboard.ping` as the window's. The phone doesn't run the reducer: it numbers its batches in the epoch its snapshot came with, keeps them until they're acknowledged and sends them again after a reconnect, and fetches a fresh snapshot (`whiteboard.sync`) after a committed patch while the board is on screen. The live card is a page bundled in the app that frames `/whiteboard/c/<capability>/<path>` with the window's sandbox flags and speaks the window's bridge to the SDK; the app fetches those files with its own client and answers a video's byte ranges itself, and fetches pictures from `/api/whiteboard/file` with its device token. Thumbnails, and the fallback card, come from `/api/whiteboard/render`, so without the office's Chromium the grid shows why there are none.

**Terminal views.** The phone runs a real terminal emulator, so a worker's terminal keeps its scrollback: swipe to read earlier lines, and **Latest** goes back to the bottom. In a full-screen program a swipe scrolls the program instead. There are two views. A worker's terminal opens in **Phone**, which changes the PTY's columns and rows to fit readable text on the phone (the keyboard changes only the rows), so programs wrap and redraw at that size, and desktop views see the same grid. Tap **Phone** in the quick keys to switch to **Desktop**, which shows the terminal at your desktop's size, scaled to fit the phone, and never resizes it; the size from before comes back, as it does when you leave the terminal. A cloud worker has no terminal there and stays on Desktop. If a desktop window resizes the terminal meanwhile, the phone switches to Desktop and leaves that size alone. If the phone drops off while in Phone (out of range, the app closed), the office puts the size back itself.

**Pairing.** Open ⚙️ Settings → **Phone & web** and choose **📱 Pair a phone** (also in the **☰** menu and the command palette). The window shows a QR code, the addresses it carries and the phones already paired. Scan the code with the app. A few seconds later the phone shows up under **Paired phones**, marked *Just paired*. The window only opens on the office's own machine (its browser on `localhost`, or the Mac app): a phone or another browser on the LAN can't read the code or the list. The app also pairs from the join URL's QR code in the office's terminal; the terminal lists the Tailscale join URLs under it.

The QR code holds a link like this, every value URL-encoded:

```
droidoffice://pair?v=1&name=<this machine's name>&t=<this start's LAN token>&u=http://192.168.1.20:4600&u=http://my-mac.tailnet-name.ts.net:4600&u=http://100.101.102.103:4600
```

The app sends `POST /api/mobile/pair?t=<token>` with `{"device": "Pixel 10 Pro XL"}` to the first address that answers, and gets back `{"deviceId", "token", "office": {"name", "version"}}`. From then on it uses that device token, not the LAN token, so it stays paired when the office restarts and the LAN token changes. `GET /api/mobile/hello` checks an address and a token quickly. Hiring from the phone sends `worker.spawn` with `deskId: "auto"`, which seats the worker at the first free desk on the floor (then the first free bean bag).

**Wi-Fi and Tailscale.** The code lists the office's Wi-Fi (LAN) addresses first, then its Tailscale ones, labelled in the window: the MagicDNS name (`my-mac.tailnet-name.ts.net`) and the `100.x.y.z` address, read from `tailscale status --json` (the `tailscale` command on `PATH`, or the one inside `/Applications/Tailscale.app`). Wi-Fi works while the phone is on the same network as the office's machine. Tailscale works from anywhere once the phone is signed in to the same tailnet, with no ports opened to the internet. Without Tailscale, or signed out, the code just has the Wi-Fi addresses. An office started with `--host 127.0.0.1` can't be reached by a phone at all, and the window says so instead of showing a code. With `--self-signed` or `--tls-cert`, the addresses are `https://`.

**Desktop browser.** The same office also has a web companion at `/dashboard` on its existing address, for a desktop browser rather than a phone. Open 🌐 **Web companion** from the ☰ menu (or ⚙️ Settings → **Phone & web** → Web companion). Choose **Open here** on the hosting computer (no pairing needed), or **Copy pairing link** for Wi-Fi or Tailscale and paste the complete link into the other computer’s browser. Tailscale is offered only while it is running; otherwise the window links to its download. The copy buttons include the private access key, so the window does not show bare addresses for manual entry. Choose **Pair this browser** before refreshing. Opening a bare address does not send an approval request to the hosting computer. Pairing stores a device token for this browser at this exact origin. Refreshing or bookmarking `/dashboard` loads a page with no office data, then uses the saved token; the LAN token is only for that first open. **Forget here** (in the page's access dialog) removes the saved token and leaves the server pairing. **Unpair** in the Web companion window removes it on the office and closes its tabs. A browser paired on a LAN IP is not paired on a Tailscale name, or the other way around. The switch at the top of the window turns the web companion off: no browser can pair, paired browsers are refused and their open tabs show that it's turned off, and they work again when it's back on. Phones are not affected. The host has to be awake and running the office. Closing the Mac app stops the server. Current Chrome, Chromium, Edge, Firefox and Safari are the supported desktop browsers. There is no phone layout, installable app, notification, or offline mode.

**Forgetting a phone.** **Forget** next to a device in the Phone window (click it twice to be sure) unpairs it: its token stops working at once, and any connection it has open is closed. Unpairing in the app does the same from the phone (`DELETE /api/mobile/pair` with its token). Lost a phone? Forget it here. Pair it again any time by scanning the code.

**Names on the phone.** Under **Me**, **Rename** on an office changes the name this phone shows. The office keeps its own name, and nothing is sent back. The name you chose stays through app updates and reconnects. Leave it blank to show the office's name again. Pairing that office again keeps it too.

**Device tokens.** Each pairing mints a random 256-bit token, sent to the phone once. The office writes only its SHA-256, the phone's name and when it paired and was last seen to `devices.json` in the office's own `.droid-office/` (next to `subagents.json`), with mode 0600, and it compares tokens in constant time. *Last seen* is written at most once a minute. Up to 32 phones can be paired at once. Like the LAN token, a device token is as good as a shell on the office's machine: anyone holding one can drive Droid there.
